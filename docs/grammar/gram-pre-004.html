1Concept Introduction — The Shape of a Syllable Block

In GRAM-PRE-001 you learned that a Hangul syllable block is built from an initial consonant (초성), a vowel (중성), and an optional final consonant (종성 / 받침). In GRAM-PRE-002 and GRAM-PRE-003 you learned every vowel and every consonant you need. Now it's time to answer a question those chapters left open: once you know which letters go into a block, exactly where inside the square does each one sit?

The answer depends entirely on the shape of the vowel you are using. Some vowels are drawn as a tall vertical line, so they sit beside the initial consonant. Other vowels are drawn as a flat horizontal line, so they sit underneath it. Getting this layout right is not just a matter of good handwriting — it is what makes a block readable as a single Korean syllable at all.

Preview
가 (ga), 고 (go), and 과 (gwa) all start with the exact same initial consonant ㄱ — yet the three blocks look completely different in shape. That difference comes entirely from how each vowel is oriented inside the square, which is exactly what this chapter explains.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Recap — three slots, two of them required

As a one-line recap of GRAM-PRE-001: every syllable block = 초성 (initial, required) + 중성 (vowel, required) + 종성/받침 (final, optional). A block can never skip the initial or the vowel, but many perfectly ordinary blocks — like 가 or 고 — simply have no final consonant at all.

SlotKorean nameRequired?
Initial consonant초성Required (silent ㅇ if the syllable "starts with a vowel")
Vowel중성Required — every block has exactly one
Final consonant종성 / 받침Optional
