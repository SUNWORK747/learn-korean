1Concept Introduction — Setting the Scene, or Softening a "But"

This is the first grammar point of Intermediate (중급, TOPIK II 3–4급) Korean, picking up right after the 39 Elementary items you just finished. -는데 is one of the most frequently used connective endings in natural spoken Korean — native speakers use it constantly to link two clauses in a way that either sets the stage for what they're about to say, or gently signals a contrast without sounding as blunt as "but." Unlike the Elementary connectives -고 (simple listing) or -지만 (direct contrast), -는데 carries a subtler, conversational feel that's hard to fully translate but instantly recognizable once you start noticing it.

The good news is that the meaning really does split into just two main jobs: (1) giving background information before making a related comment, and (2) introducing a mild, sometimes unexpected contrast. The trickier part is the form, since -는데/-(으)ㄴ데/인데 changes shape depending on whether you're attaching it to a verb, an adjective, or 이다, and whether the tense is present or past. This chapter walks through both the form rules and the two meanings side by side.

Preview
저는 학생인데, 시간이 별로 없어요. ("I'm a student, but I don't have much free time.")
어제 친구를 만났는데, 정말 반가웠어요. ("I met a friend yesterday, and it was really nice to see them.")
학생인데 (이다 + -는데) sets up a mild contrast before the comment about limited time. 만났는데 (past tense of 만나다 + -는데) instead sets the scene — the background — before describing how the speaker felt. Same ending, two different jobs.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: it depends on word type and tense

-는데/-(으)ㄴ데/인데 is really one ending with several faces. Verbs (and the special pair 있다/없다) take -는데 in the present tense; adjectives take -(으)ㄴ데; and 이다/아니다 take 인데/아닌데. In the past tense, everything simplifies to the same -았/었는데 pattern you already know from -았/었- (GRAM-BEG-015).

-는데/-(으)ㄴ데/인데 by word type and tense
Word typePresentPastExample
Verb (동사)-는데-았/었는데가는데 / 갔는데
있다/없다-는데-았/었는데있는데 / 없었는데
Adjective (형용사)-(으)ㄴ데-았/었는데예쁜데 / 예뻤는데
이다/아니다인데/아닌데이었는데/였는데학생인데 / 학생이었는데
Study Tip 있다/없다 behave like verbs and take -는데 (있는데, 없는데) — not -(으)ㄴ데 — even though "to have/be" can feel adjective-like in English. This also affects "있다-compound" adjectives like 맛있다 ("to be tasty") and 재미있다 ("to be fun"): they conjugate like 있다, so it's 맛있는데 and 재미있는데, never 맛있은데.
