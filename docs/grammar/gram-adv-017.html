-(으)ㄴ/는 듯하다 Formal Literary Conjecture

"It Seems As Though..." — Guessing in the Register of Essays and News

Learning Goals ① Understand that -(으)ㄴ/는/(으)ㄹ 듯하다 expresses a soft conjecture — "it seems as though / it appears that" — built on the dependent noun 듯 ("as if, like") + 하다, and recognize this as a 문어체 (written/literary register) form typical of essays, news articles, and formal or academic writing
② Correctly form -는 듯하다 (present/ongoing), -(으)ㄴ 듯하다 (past/completed, or an adjective's present state), and -(으)ㄹ 듯하다 (future/likely) for verbs, adjectives, 이다, and 있다/없다, and recognize the related adverbial connective -듯이 ("as if...")
③ Compare -듯하다 with the everyday conversational -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 (GRAM-INT-012): the two share essentially the same core meaning, but differ sharply in register — -듯하다 belongs to formal writing, while -것 같다 is the default choice in ordinary conversation

1Concept Introduction — A Guess Dressed for Writing

This chapter continues the "추측·전문(傳聞) 고급 표현" (Advanced Conjecture/Hearsay Expressions) cluster, right after GRAM-ADV-016 -(으)ㄹ 리가 없다 showed you how to reject a possibility outright. -(으)ㄴ/는/(으)ㄹ 듯하다 is a much gentler tool: it makes an ordinary, hedged guess — "it seems as though," "it appears that" — the same basic job that -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 (GRAM-INT-012) already does for you in everyday conversation. What's new here isn't the meaning; it's the register. -듯하다 is the version of that same soft guess that belongs in an essay, a news report, an official document, or academic writing — the written, literary register Korean calls 문어체.

The pattern is built from the dependent noun 듯, meaning "as if" or "like," followed by 하다. A modifier attaches to 듯 the same way one attaches to 것 in -것 같다, so once you know -것 같다's formation, -듯하다's formation will feel very familiar — the two patterns are near-mirror images of each other in shape, and nearly identical in meaning. The real skill this chapter teaches is knowing when to reach for the formal one instead of the conversational one.

Preview
최근 소비자들의 관심이 높아진 듯하다. ("It appears that consumer interest has recently increased.")
그는 이 결정에 만족하는 듯하다. ("He seems to be satisfied with this decision.")
Both sentences read like lines from a news article or report — a careful, slightly detached way of stating a conclusion the writer believes but doesn't claim to know for certain. In casual speech, a friend would say the very same thing with -것 같아요 instead.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 The origin: 듯 ("as if, like") + 하다

듯 is a dependent noun (it never stands alone) that means roughly "as if" or "like" — the same root that appears in the connective -듯이 (see 2.4 below). -(으)ㄴ/는/(으)ㄹ 듯하다 attaches a modifier to 듯 and then closes the phrase with 하다, here used in its general sense of "to be/to seem" rather than "to do." Read piece by piece, 오는 듯하다 works out to "[the situation] is like [it's coming]" — i.e., "it seems as though it's coming." Structurally this is the same design as -것 같다 (것 "thing" + 같다 "to be alike"): a bound noun standing in for "the situation," modified by what you're guessing, then closed off by a word meaning "to seem."

Study Tip Because 듯 and 것 both function as placeholder nouns for "the situation as it appears," you can treat -듯하다 as -것 같다's formal twin: swap 것 같다 for 듯하다 (adjusting spacing — 듯하다 is often written as one word) and the meaning barely changes, but the sentence instantly reads more like writing than speech.
