Palatalization 구개음화

Basic Pronunciation Rules — When ㄷ/ㅌ Meet the Vowel 이

GRAM-PRE-010 Beginner (Pre-TOPIK) 15 min
LevelBeginner (for students with no prior knowledge of Hangul)
CategoryHangul > 1. Hangul (Alphabet & Pronunciation)
Learning Goals ① State the palatalization rule: batchim ㄷ or ㅌ becomes [ㅈ] or [ㅊ] when directly followed by 이 (or 히, which combines aspiration and palatalization) in the next syllable
② Apply the rule to read real words correctly
③ Recognize that this rule is specifically triggered by the vowel 이, which sets it apart from the other pronunciation rules in this unit (which are triggered by neighboring consonants)

1Concept Introduction — A Rule Triggered by a Vowel, Not a Consonant

So far in this unit, every pronunciation rule you've met has been triggered by two consonants bumping into each other across a syllable boundary: linking moves a batchim into an empty next slot, nasalization softens a batchim before ㄴ/ㅁ, and aspiration fuses a batchim with a neighboring ㅎ. Palatalization (구개음화) is different — it is the one rule in this group that is triggered by a vowel: specifically, the vowel 이.

When a syllable ending in batchim ㄷ or ㅌ is immediately followed by a syllable that starts with 이, the batchim doesn't simply link over as "d" or "t" — it shifts into a completely different sound, [ㅈ] or [ㅊ]. This chapter will show you the formula, the special "combined" case with 히, and why the vowel 이 has this unique power over ㄷ/ㅌ.

Preview
같이 (written gat-i, "together") is never pronounced "gat-i." Because batchim ㅌ is directly followed by 이, it is pronounced [가치] (ga-chi). Learn this one rule and you'll never misread this extremely common word again.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 The rule: batchim ㄷ/ㅌ + 이 → [ㅈ]/[ㅊ]

When batchim ㄷ or ㅌ is directly followed by the vowel 이 at the start of the next syllable, the two sounds merge into a single palatal consonant sound carried into that next syllable.

The core palatalization formula
BatchimFollowed byPronounced asExample
ㄷ이[ㅈ]굳이 → [구지]
ㅌ이[ㅊ]같이 → [가치]
Key Rule This shift only happens when 이 lands immediately after the batchim, across a syllable boundary — most commonly when a grammar particle or suffix beginning with 이 is attached to a word ending in ㄷ or ㅌ. You don't need to memorize which suffixes trigger it yet — just recognize the pattern ㄷ/ㅌ + 이 → ㅈ/ㅊ whenever you see it.
