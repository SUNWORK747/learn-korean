1Concept Introduction — When a Fact Leaves No Way Around the Conclusion

This chapter opens a new cluster of advanced expressions built around cause and condition (인과·조건 고급 표현, GRAM-ADV-012∼015). You already met one advanced conditional, -는 한 (GRAM-ADV-003, "as long as / provided that"): it marks a boundary that must keep holding for a result to keep holding — the moment the condition stops, so does the guarantee. -는 이상 is a different, stronger tool. It doesn't describe a boundary that might change; it takes something as an already-settled, unshakeable fact and asserts that a certain conclusion or obligation inevitably follows from it — there is no way around it. English renders this as "given that…," "since this is the case…," or "now that…, [it follows that]."

-는 이상 attaches to the present/ongoing-state stem of a verb, just like the -는 you already know from -는 한 or -는 것. But -는 이상 also has a completed-fact counterpart that -는 한 lacks: -(으)ㄹ 이상 is not used — instead, an already-happened or already-decided action takes -(으)ㄴ 이상 (the ordinary past-tense attributive), and a noun predicate takes 인 이상. This chapter covers all three forms, the core meaning of inevitable necessity, and a direct comparison with -는 한 so you can choose the right tool with confidence.

Preview
학생인 이상 공부를 열심히 해야 한다. ("Given that you are a student, you have no choice but to study hard.")
이왕 시작한 이상 끝까지 해내야 한다. ("Since you've already started, you have to see it through to the end.")
학생인 이상 treats "being a student" as a fixed fact (Noun + 인 이상) and draws an unavoidable duty from it: study hard, no way around it. 시작한 이상 treats "having already started" as a completed, unchangeable fact (verb + -(으)ㄴ 이상), from which finishing becomes the necessary conclusion. Both main clauses lean on an obligation ending, -어야 하다.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: -는 이상 (ongoing) vs. -(으)ㄴ 이상 (established fact) vs. 인 이상 (noun)

-는 이상 attaches to the present/ongoing-state stem of a verb — the same -는 attributive used in -는 한, -는 것 (GRAM-BEG-038), and -는데 (GRAM-INT-001) — and is used when the fact or state is currently true / ongoing (e.g. 근무하는 이상, "given that you currently work [here]"). 있다/없다 behave like verbs and also take -는 이상. When the fact is instead something that has already happened or already been decided and can no longer be undone, the verb takes the ordinary past-tense attributive, -(으)ㄴ 이상 (e.g. 시작한 이상, "given that you have already started"; 결정한 이상, "given that it has already been decided"). A noun predicate uses 인 이상 (학생 → 학생인 이상).

-는 이상 / -(으)ㄴ 이상 / 인 이상 by word type

Verb (ongoing/present state)-는 이상근무하다 → 근무하는 이상Fact/state true right now

결정하다 → 결정한 이상		Already happened, cannot be undone

Study Tip To choose between -는 이상 and -(으)ㄴ 이상, ask: "Is this fact still ongoing right now, or is it something that already happened and is now fixed/settled?" 근무하는 이상 = "given that you currently work here" (ongoing state). 시작한 이상 = "given that you have already started" (a completed, unchangeable fact). You'll often see -(으)ㄴ 이상 introduced by the adverb 이왕 ("since it's already the case that…"), as in 이왕 온 이상 ("since you've already come…") — a very common collocation worth memorizing as a set phrase.
