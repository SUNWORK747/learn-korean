-고 있다 Present Progressive ("-ing")

Describing an Action That Is Happening Right Now

GRAM-BEG-033 Elementary (TOPIK I) 20 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — first item of this stage's Other Essential Expressions section
CategoryOther Essential Expressions > 5. Other Essential Expressions
Learning Goals ① Attach -고 있다/있어요 to a verb stem to describe an action currently in progress ("is/am/are ~ing"), with no batchim rule needed for -고 itself
② Conjugate 있다 as 있어요/있습니다 to use -고 있다 naturally in both statements and questions
③ Recognize that -고 있다 marks an action happening right now, which is different from a plain -아/어요 statement of habitual or general fact

1Concept Introduction — What's Happening Right Now

With this chapter you move into a brand-new section, "Other Essential Expressions," which gathers several extremely common patterns that don't fit neatly into "particle," "sentence-final ending," or "connective ending" — but that you'll use constantly in real conversation. First up is -고 있다, the standard way to say an action is in progress right now, the closest Korean equivalent to the English "-ing" (as in "I am eating").

You've actually already seen the syllable 고 attach to a verb stem without any batchim rule — in GRAM-BEG-022 -고 싶다 ("want to"). -고 있다 reuses that exact same unchanging -고, just followed by a different second verb (있다, "to exist/be," instead of 싶다, "to want"). If you're comfortable with -고 싶다's formation, -고 있다 will feel very familiar.

Preview
저는 지금 밥을 먹고 있어요. ("I am eating right now.")
뭐 하고 있어요? ("What are you doing?")
The unchanging -고 attaches straight to the verb stem, and 있어요 (a form of "to exist") shows the action is happening right now, in progress.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Attaching -고 있다/있어요 — no batchim rule needed

-고 있다 attaches directly after a verb stem: verb stem + -고 있다 (dictionary/plain form) or verb stem + -고 있어요 (polite casual, 해요체). Just like -고 싶다, the -고 itself never changes — it attaches the same way whether the stem ends in a consonant or a vowel.

Verb stem + -고 있어요 — the same ending every time
Verb (dictionary form)Stem+ -고 있어요Meaning
먹다 (to eat)먹 (consonant-ending)먹고 있어요"is/am/are eating"
가다 (to go)가 (vowel-ending)가고 있어요"is/am/are going"
공부하다 (to study)공부하 (vowel-ending)공부하고 있어요"is/am/are studying"
읽다 (to read)읽 (consonant-ending)읽고 있어요"is/am/are reading"

The 있다 part conjugates like any regular verb: polite casual 있어요, formal 있습니다, and simply add a question mark (있어요? / 있습니까?) to ask whether someone is currently doing something.

Study Tip Compare the two "-고 + verb" patterns you now know: -고 싶다 (GRAM-BEG-022, "want to") and -고 있다 (this chapter, "is ~ing"). Same unchanging -고, completely different second verb, completely different meaning. Always check which second verb follows -고 before translating.

3Examples

3.1 -고 있어요 breaking down a question, syllable by syllable

지금now
뭐what
하do-stem
고-go
있exist-stem
어요-eoyo?

지금 뭐 하고 있어요? ("What are you doing right now?") — 하다 (do) becomes stem 하, adds the unchanging -고, then 있어요 shows the action is in progress at this moment.
