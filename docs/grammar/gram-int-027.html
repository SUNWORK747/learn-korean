-(이)라도 Settling for the Next-Best Option

When the Ideal Choice Isn't Available, X Will Do

GRAM-INT-027 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 4th item of the "조사·의존명사 확장" (Particles & Bound Nouns Expansion) section, right after GRAM-INT-026 -(이)나
CategoryParticles & Bound Nouns Expansion > 5. 조사·의존명사 확장
Learning Goals ① Understand that -(이)라도 attaches to a noun to mark it as a fallback, "second-best" choice — acceptable, though not the speaker's ideal pick — using 이라도 after a consonant-final noun and 라도 after a vowel-final noun
② Recognize and correctly use the 아무 + (이)라도 pattern ("anyone/anything/anywhere at all will do"), an intensified version of the same fallback meaning
③ Distinguish -(이)라도 from -(이)나 (GRAM-INT-026): -(이)라도 specifically implies settling for a non-ideal option because nothing better is available, while -(이)나 is a more neutral "or/selection" particle that doesn't carry that nuance

1Concept Introduction — Settling for the Next-Best Option

This item continues directly from GRAM-INT-026 -(이)나, and the two are easy to mix up because both can be loosely translated as "or" or "even" in English. -(이)라도 is a particle that attaches to a noun and marks it as a fallback choice — not the speaker's first pick, but an acceptable substitute given the circumstances. The closest English equivalents are "even X (will do)," "X, at least," or "how about X, since nothing better is available." Every time you see -(이)라도, there's an unspoken sense that the ideal option is missing, unknown, or out of reach, and the noun it attaches to is the next best thing.

The form is simple — 이라도 after a consonant-final noun (받침 있음) and 라도 after a vowel-final noun (받침 없음), the same batchim-based split you already know from 이에요/예요. Beyond the plain form, this chapter also covers the emphatic 아무 + (이)라도 pattern ("anyone/anything/anywhere at all will do"), and closes with a direct comparison between -(이)라도 and the previous item -(이)나, since telling the two apart is the trickiest part of this chapter.

Preview
물이라도 주세요. ("Please at least give me some water" — nothing else is available, so water will do.)
시간이 없으면 문자라도 보내 주세요. ("If you don't have time, please at least send me a text.")
아무거라도 좋으니까 빨리 먹자. ("Anything at all is fine, so let's eat quickly.")
물이라도 (consonant-final 물 + 이라도) and 문자라도 (vowel-final 문자 + 라도) both signal "this isn't the ideal option, but it's acceptable." 아무거라도 pushes that same idea to its extreme: not just one fallback, but literally any option at all.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: 이라도 after a consonant, 라도 after a vowel

Like most Korean particles, -(이)라도 changes shape depending on whether the noun it attaches to ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel. It also behaves like -밖에, -조차, and -마저 (GRAM-INT-024, GRAM-INT-025) in that it can replace 을/를 outright, or stack after other particles.

-(이)라도 attachment patterns
Noun endingFormExample
Consonant-final (받침 있음)이라도물이라도, 사람이라도, 학생이라도
Vowel-final (받침 없음)라도커피라도, 의자라도, 학교라도
Replacing 을/를Noun + 이라도/라도 (을/를 dropped)밥을 먹다 → 밥이라도 먹자
Stacking after other particlesNoun + 에서/한테/까지 + (이)라도여기서라도, 오늘까지라도
Study Tip Don't confuse the particle -(이)라도 (attaches to a noun, covered in this chapter) with the unrelated-looking verb/adjective ending -더라도 ("even if," a later Advanced item). They share the -라도 syllables and both carry a "settling/even" flavor, but -(이)라도 always follows a noun, while -더라도 follows a verb or adjective stem. For now, just remember: noun + (이)라도.
