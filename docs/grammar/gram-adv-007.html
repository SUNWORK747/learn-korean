1Concept Introduction — No Matter What Stands in the Way

Welcome to a brand-new category: 양보·대조 고급 표현 (Advanced Concessive/Contrast Expressions). Over the next five chapters (GRAM-ADV-007 through GRAM-ADV-011), you'll learn five different ways that educated, formal Korean expresses "even if / although" — each with its own shade of meaning, formality, and emotional color. -(으)ㄹ지라도 opens this cluster because it is the strongest and most dramatic of the five: it doesn't just concede a point quietly, it makes a bold, almost defiant declaration that the outcome will hold true no matter what obstacle or hardship stands in the way. Every later chapter in this cluster will be compared back to this one, so it's worth getting comfortable with it here first.

You've likely already met the basic concessive -아/어도 ("even if / even though") in everyday conversational Korean — a sentence like 바빠도 갈게요 ("Even though I'm busy, I'll go"). -(으)ㄹ지라도 expresses that same core logical relationship, but dialed up several notches: it belongs to 문어체 (the written, literary register) used in essays, speeches, news articles, and official documents, and it frequently teams up with intensifying adverbs like 아무리 ("no matter how"), 비록, and 설령 ("even if / granted that") to build sentences that sound resolute and dramatic. You'll rarely hear it in casual conversation between friends — save it for writing and formal speech.

Preview
아무리 어려울지라도 포기하지 않겠습니다. ("No matter how difficult it may be, I will not give up.")
비록 실패할지라도 다시 도전해야 한다. ("Even if [I] fail, [I] must try again.")
어려울지라도 (어렵다, a ㅂ-irregular adjective, + -(으)ㄹ지라도) and 실패할지라도 (실패하다, a vowel-final verb stem, + -ㄹ지라도) both carry the bold, formal tone typical of written Korean. Notice how 아무리 and 비록 sharpen the emphasis — a pairing you'll see constantly with this grammar point.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: stem + -(으)ㄹ지라도

-(으)ㄹ지라도 attaches directly to verb and adjective stems, and to 이다/아니다. Like other -(으)ㄹ-family endings, the 으 appears only when the stem ends in a consonant; a vowel-final stem takes -ㄹ지라도 directly, and a stem that already ends in ㄹ simply adds -지라도 (the extra ㄹ is never doubled). There is no separate past-tense shape built into the ending itself — to concede a completed or already-decided situation, -았/었- is inserted before it, giving -았/었을지라도 (e.g. 실패했을지라도, "even if it turns out [I] have failed"), though this combination is far less common than the plain form shown below.

-(으)ㄹ지라도 by stem type
Stem typeRuleExample
Vowel-final stemstem + -ㄹ지라도가다 → 갈지라도, 바쁘다 → 바쁠지라도
Consonant-final stemstem + -을지라도먹다 → 먹을지라도, 많다 → 많을지라도
ㄹ-final stemstem + -지라도 (no extra ㄹ/으)살다 → 살지라도, 알다 → 알지라도
ㅂ irregularㅂ → 우 + -ㄹ지라도어렵다 → 어려울지라도
ㄷ irregularㄷ → ㄹ + -을지라도걷다 → 걸을지라도
이다/아니다이/아니 + -ㄹ지라도학생이다 → 학생일지라도, 사실이 아니다 → 사실이 아닐지라도
Study Tip 이다 becomes 일지라도 for every noun, whether or not it ends in a batchim — 학생일지라도 (학생 has a batchim) and 친구일지라도 (친구 does not) follow the exact same pattern, just like 것이다 → 것일 거예요. Also watch for the ㄹ-final rule: because -(으)ㄹ지라도 already starts with the same ㄹ sound that ㄹ-final stems end in, only one ㄹ ever appears — 살다 becomes 살지라도, never 살ㄹ지라도.
