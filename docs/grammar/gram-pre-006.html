How to Write Hangul Letters Correctly

GRAM-PRE-006 Beginner (Pre-TOPIK) 15 min
LevelBeginner (for students who already recognize Hangul letters and want to write them correctly)
CategoryHangul > 1. Hangul (Alphabet & Pronunciation)
Learning Goals ① State the 4 general stroke-order principles of Hangul
② Know the correct number and order of strokes for the 14 basic consonants and 10 basic vowels
③ Apply correct stroke order when writing a complete syllable block (initial → vowel → final)

1Concept Introduction — Why Stroke Order Matters

You already know what the Hangul letters look like and how they sound. But how you draw each letter — the order and direction of the individual pen strokes — also matters. Writing with the correct stroke order makes your handwriting more legible, more consistent, and closer to how native speakers naturally write. It also builds muscle memory that makes writing faster and neater over time, the same way learning the correct stroke order for Chinese characters or Japanese kana helps learners of those languages.

Unlike a grammar pattern, stroke order does not have hundreds of "rules" to memorize — Hangul follows just 4 simple general principles that apply to almost every letter. In this chapter you will first learn those 4 principles, then see the specific stroke count and stroke order for each of the 14 basic consonants and 10 basic vowels, and finally see how a complete syllable block is assembled stroke-by-stroke using the block 한 (han) as a worked example.

Preview
The block 한 (han) is written with 3 jamo — ㅎ, ㅏ, ㄴ — always in that order: initial consonant → vowel → final consonant. Within each jamo, you also follow a fixed stroke order. By the end of this chapter, you will be able to write out the full numbered stroke sequence for any basic Hangul letter.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 The 4 general stroke-order principles

Before looking at individual letters, learn these 4 principles. Almost every letter in Hangul can be written correctly just by applying them in order.

4 general principles of Hangul stroke order
#PrincipleWhat it means
1Left to rightStrokes that run horizontally, or letters made of side-by-side parts, are written starting from the left and moving right.
2Top to bottomStrokes that run vertically, or letters made of stacked parts, are written starting from the top and moving down.
3Outer before inner (enclosing shapes)For box-like or enclosing letters (ㅁ, ㅇ, ㅂ), the outer frame strokes are written before the shape is fully closed.
4Horizontal before verticalWhen a horizontal stroke and a vertical stroke cross or meet at a corner, the horizontal stroke is generally written first. Each letter is generally started from its top-left-most point.
Key Rule These 4 principles are general tendencies, not rigid laws for every single letter — but if you are ever unsure how to start a new letter, applying "top-left first, left-to-right, top-to-bottom, horizontal before vertical" will almost always give you a natural, legible result.

3Examples

3.1 Worked example — writing the block 한 (han) stroke by stroke

A full syllable block is always written jamo by jamo, in the fixed order initial consonant → vowel → final consonant, and within each jamo the strokes follow the sequence you learned in §2.2 and §2.3. Here is the complete block 한 (han), broken into its 3 jamo:

ㅏvowel, 2 strokes

Full stroke sequence for 한 (6 strokes total)
Stroke #JamoRoleWhat is drawn
1ㅎinitialsmall top dash
2ㅎinitialshort horizontal stroke beneath the dash
3ㅎinitialcircular stroke at the bottom
4ㅏvowelvertical line, top to bottom
5ㅏvowelshort horizontal stroke to the right
6ㄴfinalone continuous stroke: down, then across to the right
Example
Notice that every stroke belonging to ㅎ is finished before you start ㅏ, and every stroke of ㅏ is finished before you start ㄴ. You never interleave strokes from two different jamo.
