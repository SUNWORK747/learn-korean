Talking About the Future — Plans and Guesses

GRAM-BEG-016 Elementary (TOPIK I) 20 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급)
CategorySentence-Final Endings > 2. Sentence-Final Endings
Learning Goals ① Attach -(으)ㄹ 거예요 correctly by batchim — ㄹ 거예요 after a vowel-ending stem (or drop-and-replace for ㄹ-ending stems), 을 거예요 after a consonant-ending stem
② Use -(으)ㄹ 거예요 to express the SPEAKER's future plan/intention
③ Use -(으)ㄹ 거예요 to express PRESUMPTION/guesses about situations or other people, and recognize its real pronunciation [꺼예요] due to tensification (GRAM-PRE-011)

1Concept Introduction — Talking About What Hasn't Happened Yet

So far you've learned how to talk about the present with -아/어요 (GRAM-BEG-013) and about the past with -았/었- (GRAM-BEG-015). The one time frame still missing is the future — and that's exactly what -(으)ㄹ 거예요 is for. It attaches to a verb or adjective stem and turns a plain statement into one about something that hasn't happened yet.

-(으)ㄹ 거예요 actually does two related jobs. First, it expresses the speaker's own plan or intention — "I will/am going to ___." Second, it expresses a presumption or guess — "[someone/something] will probably ___," used for anyone or anything, including the weather. Both meanings share the same form; you tell them apart from context, which you'll practice in this chapter.

Preview
저는 내일 갈 거예요. ("I will go tomorrow.") — the speaker's own plan.
아마 비가 올 거예요. ("It will probably rain.") — a guess about something outside anyone's control. Same ending, two different jobs — that's the core of this chapter.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Attaching -(으)ㄹ 거예요 — the batchim rule

-(으)ㄹ 거예요 attaches to a verb or adjective stem (the dictionary form minus 다). Which shape you use depends on whether the stem ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel — the same batchim logic you've now seen across several endings.

-(으)ㄹ 거예요 — attachment rule
Stem endingFormExampleMeaning
Vowel (받침 없음)+ ㄹ 거예요가다 → 갈 거예요"will go"
Consonant (받침 있음)+ 을 거예요먹다 → 먹을 거예요"will eat"
Consonant (받침 있음)+ 을 거예요읽다 → 읽을 거예요"will read"
ㄹ-ending stem (irregular)drop ㄹ, + ㄹ 거예요살다 → 살 거예요"will live"
Study Tip Stems that already end in ㄹ (like 살다 "to live" or 만들다 "to make") follow the special ㄹ-drop pattern seen throughout Korean conjugation: drop the stem's final ㄹ, then add ㄹ 거예요. Because both pieces are ㄹ, the result looks unchanged — 살다 → 살 거예요, 만들다 → 만들 거예요 — but grammatically the drop-and-replace still happened underneath.
