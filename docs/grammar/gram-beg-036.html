-아/어야 하다/되다 Obligation ("Must / Have To")

Saying What You Must Do

GRAM-BEG-036 Elementary (TOPIK I) 20 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — 2nd item of this stage's Essential Expressions section
CategoryEssential Expressions > 5. Essential Expressions
Learning Goals ① Attach -아/어야 하다/되다 to a verb or adjective stem to express obligation ("must / have to"), using the same bright/dark vowel-harmony rule as -아/어요, including the 하다-irregular case (해야 하다)
② Recognize that -아/어야 하다 and -아/어야 되다 are near-interchangeable in meaning, with 되다 sounding slightly more casual/spoken
③ Recognize the opposite meaning, -지 않아도 되다 ("don't have to / it's okay not to"), as the "no obligation" counterpart to this pattern

1Concept Introduction — Saying What You Must Do

So far you've learned how to describe actions in progress (-고 있다), states that persist (-아/어 있다), and how to politely ask someone for something (-아/어 주세요). This chapter adds a new kind of sentence: expressing that something is necessary or required — "I must go," "You have to study," "The room has to be clean." In English this idea is carried by separate words like "must" or "have to." In Korean, it's built directly onto the verb or adjective stem with the ending -아/어야 하다 (or its close cousin -아/어야 되다).

The good news: the -아/어야 part follows the exact same bright-vowel/dark-vowel rule you already mastered in GRAM-BEG-013 (-아/어요). You're not learning a new conjugation system here — you're reusing a rule you already know and simply attaching a new meaning ("must") on top of it with 하다 ("to do") or 되다 ("to become/be OK").

Preview
저는 지금 가야 해요. ("I have to go now.")
숙제를 먼저 해야 돼요. ("You have to do your homework first.")
Both sentences express obligation. The ending changes shape (아야/어야) depending on the verb's vowel, but the meaning is always "must / have to."

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Attaching -아/어야 하다/되다 — the same vowel-harmony rule as -아/어요

Take the verb or adjective stem, check its last vowel, and choose -아야 (bright vowels —ㅏ, ㅗ) or -어야 (everything else), then add 하다 or 되다. The irregular verb 하다 ("to do") itself becomes 해야 하다, exactly as it became 해요 back in GRAM-BEG-013.

Stem + -아/어야 하다 — same vowel-harmony rule as -아/어요
Verb/AdjectiveStem+ -아/어야 하다Meaning
가다 (to go)가 (ㅏ, bright)가야 하다"must go"
오다 (to come)오 (ㅗ, bright)와야 하다"must come"
먹다 (to eat)먹 (ㅓ, dark)먹어야 하다"must eat"
읽다 (to read)읽 (ㅣ, dark)읽어야 하다"must read"
하다 (to do) — irregular하 → 해해야 하다"must do"

In everyday polite speech (해요체), this becomes -아/어야 해요: 가야 해요, 와야 해요, 먹어야 해요, 읽어야 해요, 해야 해요.

Study Tip Think of -아/어야 하다 as "stem + (아/어야, same rule as -아/어요) + 하다 (to do)." If you can already conjugate a verb into -아/어요, you already know which vowel (아 or 어) this pattern needs — just swap the final 요 for 야 하다 in your head as a shortcut.

3Examples

3.1 -아/어야 하다/되다 across different stems

The same obligation meaning, attached to different verb/adjective stems
Verb/Adjective+ -아/어야 하다/되다Example sentence
공부하다 (to study)공부해야 해요매일 한국어를 공부해야 해요. ("I have to study Korean every day.")
일찍 일어나다 (to wake up early)일찍 일어나야 돼요내일 일찍 일어나야 돼요. ("I have to wake up early tomorrow.")
깨끗하다 (to be clean)깨끗해야 해요방이 깨끗해야 해요. ("The room has to be clean.")
기다리다 (to wait)기다려야 해요여기서 기다려야 해요. ("You have to wait here.")
