-(으)ㄴ들 Concession, "Even If... It Wouldn't Matter"

The "Even If" That Admits Defeat — a Rhetorical Concessive Built on Futility

GRAM-ADV-009 Advanced (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelAdvanced (고급, TOPIK II 5–6급) — 3rd item of the "양보·대조 고급 표현" (Advanced Concessive/Contrast Expressions) section, right after GRAM-ADV-008 -더라도
Category양보·대조 고급 표현 (Advanced Concessive/Contrast Expressions) > 2. 양보·대조 고급 표현 (3rd of 5: GRAM-ADV-007–011)
Learning Goals ① Understand -(으)ㄴ들 as a concessive ending meaning "even if / even granting [X], it still wouldn't matter/help" — unlike other "even if" endings, it implies the concession is ultimately futile or pointless, and is very often paired with a rhetorical question
② Correctly attach -(으)ㄴ들 to verb, adjective, and 이다 stems (regular, ㄹ/ㅂ/ㄷ irregulars), and recognize the -았/었은들 form for conceding something already finished
③ Distinguish -(으)ㄴ들 from -아/어도, -더라도, and -(으)ㄹ지라도 by its unique "it's futile/ pointless" nuance and its strong pull toward rhetorical-question main clauses

1Concept Introduction — Conceding the Point, Then Shrugging It Off

You now know two ways to say "even if" in formal Korean: -(으)ㄹ지라도 (GRAM-ADV-007), the dramatic, defiant one, and -더라도 (GRAM-ADV-008), the common everyday advanced one. Both of those endings grant a hypothetical and then insist the main clause holds true anyway — a confident, forward-looking stance. -(으)ㄴ들 grants a hypothetical too, but with a completely different emotional color: it says "even if [X] is/were true — so what? It wouldn't change anything, it wouldn't help, it would be pointless." Where -더라도 and -(으)ㄹ지라도 sound resolute, -(으)ㄴ들 sounds resigned, almost fatalistic. It's the concessive of futility.

Because of this built-in "what's the use?" flavor, -(으)ㄴ들 shows up constantly in essays, reflective writing, and rhetorical speech that dwells on regret, hopelessness, or things too late to change — and it is overwhelmingly paired with a rhetorical question ending (-겠어요?, -(으)ㄹ까요?, or the literary -(으)랴) rather than a plain statement. A Korean speaker reaching for -(으)ㄴ들 is usually not asking a real question — they already know the answer is "no" or "it doesn't matter," and the rhetorical question is just how Korean delivers that conclusion with extra rhetorical punch.

Preview
이제 와서 후회한들 무슨 소용이 있겠어요? ("Even if you regret it now, what use would there be?" — i.e., regretting it now changes nothing.)
아무리 돈이 많은들 행복까지 살 수 있겠어요? ("No matter how much money you have, could you really buy happiness with it?" — i.e., no, you couldn't.)
Both sentences grant the concession (regretting, having lots of money) and then use a rhetorical question to declare that it makes no real difference. This "grant it, then dismiss it as useless" shape is the signature of -(으)ㄴ들.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: the same "-(으)ㄴ" shape you already know, plus 들

-(으)ㄴ들 is built from the familiar adnominal marker -(으)ㄴ (the one you met attaching to verbs and adjectives in GRAM-INT-001 -는데/-(으)ㄴ데) plus the particle 들. Unlike -는데/-(으)ㄴ데, which uses a different shape for verbs (-는데) than for adjectives (-(으)ㄴ데), -(으)ㄴ들 uses one consistent shape for both word types — verbs and adjectives both simply take -(으)ㄴ들 on the stem. For verbs this carries a natural "having done / once it's done" nuance; for adjectives it's tense-neutral, describing a quality "however true it may be."

Study Tip Because a verb's -(으)ㄴ들 already leans past/completed in meaning (후회한들 ≈ "even having regretted it"), the extra -았/었은들 layer is reserved for a clearly counterfactual "even if it had happened" reading about a finished, alternate-past situation: 그때 갔은들 상황이 달라졌을까요? ("Even if I had gone then, would the situation have been any different?"). In casual speech you'll also hear the set pattern -다 한들 ("even granting that [someone] says/claims ~"), built from the quotative -다 + 하다 + -ㄴ들: 네가 아무리 힘이 세다 한들 나를 당해 낼 수 있겠니? ("No matter how strong you claim to be, could you really beat me?").
