-치고 No Exception, or Surprisingly For a Typical X

"Not a Single One Is Different" vs. "Unusually, For an X..."

GRAM-INT-028 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 5th item of the "조사·의존명사 확장" (Particles & Bound Nouns Expansion) section, right after GRAM-INT-027 -(이)라도 (차선)
CategoryParticles & Bound Nouns Expansion > 5. 조사·의존명사 확장
Learning Goals ① Understand that -치고 attaches to a noun to make a generalization about "a typical X," and that it splits into two related but different readings depending on the predicate that follows
② Use -치고 + negative predicate to say "there is no exception" — every member of that category, without a single exception, shares the stated quality
③ Use -치고는 + positive predicate to say "surprisingly, for a typical X..." — singling out one case as an exception to what you'd normally expect

1Concept Introduction — Two Ways to Talk About "A Typical X"

-치고 attaches directly to a noun and means roughly "for a typical X" or "as far as X goes." It sets up a category (singers, students, parents, beginners, this price range, this season) and then makes a claim about how well or badly that whole category holds up — either confirming that the rule has zero exceptions, or pointing out that one particular case is an exception to what you'd expect. English doesn't have a single word that does this job, which is why -치고 often feels tricky at first: the same particle can point in two almost opposite directions depending on what comes after it.

The key is the predicate. When -치고 is followed by a negative statement (often "없다," "못하다," or "안 ~"), it means "there's no exception" — every single member of that category shares the quality being described. When -치고는 (with the topic particle 는 attached) is followed by a positive statement, it flips to mean "surprisingly, for an X..." — the speaker is pointing out that this particular case breaks the normal expectation for its category, usually in a good way. Same core idea — "a typical member of category X" — two very different conclusions.

Preview
가수치고 노래 못하는 사람 없어요. ("There's no singer who can't sing well" — every singer, without exception, can sing.)
이 아이는 5살치고는 키가 커요. ("For a 5-year-old, this child is tall" — surprisingly tall compared to a typical 5-year-old.)
가수치고 + a negative predicate (못하는 사람 없다) makes a universal, no-exception claim about all singers. 5살치고는 + a positive predicate (키가 크다) singles out this one child as an exception to the norm for 5-year-olds. Same particle, opposite direction.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: one shape, attaches straight to any noun

-치고 is refreshingly simple in form: it attaches directly to a noun with no batchim distinction — there's only one shape, unlike particles such as -은/는 or -이/가 that change depending on whether the noun ends in a consonant or vowel. Just add -치고 (or -치고는) straight onto the noun.

Noun + -치고 / -치고는
Noun+ -치고 (no exception)+ -치고는 (surprisingly)
가수 (singer)가수치고가수치고는
학생 (student)학생치고학생치고는
초보자 (beginner)초보자치고초보자치고는
이 가격 (this price)이 가격치고이 가격치고는
Study Tip Because -치고 always ends in 고, it never changes shape for batchim — 학생치고, 부모치고, 선생님치고 all just attach the same way. The only thing that changes is whether you add 는 afterward, and that choice is what steers the meaning.
