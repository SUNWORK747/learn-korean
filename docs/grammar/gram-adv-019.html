-(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다 It Amounts To / Practically the Same As Saying

Evaluative Equivalence — Doing the Mental Math

GRAM-ADV-019 Advanced (TOPIK II, 5–6급) 30 min
LevelAdvanced (고급, TOPIK II 5–6급) — 4th and last item of the "추측·전문(傳聞) 고급 표현" (Advanced Conjecture/Hearsay Expressions) cluster, following GRAM-ADV-018 -(으)ㄹ 법하다
Category추측·전문(傳聞) 고급 표현 > 4. -(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다
Learning Goals ① Understand that -(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다 expresses the speaker's evaluative conclusion — "it amounts to / it's practically the same as saying that..." — and know that it is built on the bound noun 셈 ("calculation, reckoning")
② Correctly attach -는 셈이다 to verbs for ongoing/general situations, -(으)ㄴ 셈이다 to verbs for completed results, and -(으)ㄴ/인 셈이다 to adjectives and 이다
③ Distinguish -(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다's evaluative-summary function (the speaker already knows the real situation) from true conjecture expressions like -는 듯하다/-(으)ㄹ 법하다 (guesses about an unknown fact), and understand why it is still grouped under 추측·전문

1Concept Introduction — Rounding a Situation Off to Its Bottom Line

Every one of the guessing expressions you've studied so far — -(으)ㄹ 리가 없다, -는 듯하다, -(으)ㄹ 법하다 — deals with something the speaker does not fully know for certain. -(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다 works differently. The speaker already knows exactly what happened; what they're doing is evaluating it — standing back, doing the mental math, and announcing the practical bottom line. The core meaning is "it amounts to..." or "it's basically the same as saying that...": the literal facts might not be 100% identical to the stated conclusion, but close enough that treating them as equivalent is fair. 거의 다 왔으니까 도착한 셈이다 doesn't claim you have literally arrived — it means you're so close that, for all practical purposes, arriving is a done deal.

This is the fourth and final chapter of the "추측·전문(傳聞) 고급 표현" (Advanced Conjecture/Hearsay Expressions) cluster, GRAM-ADV-016~019, which began with -(으)ㄹ 리가 없다 and continued through -는 듯하다 and -(으)ㄹ 법하다. -(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다 sits in this cluster by tradition, but as you'll see in §2.4, it isn't really "conjecture" in the same sense as its three neighbors — it's an evaluative summary of a known situation, not a guess about an unknown one. Once this chapter is done, the entire cluster is complete, and the curriculum moves into its final category: "담화·논증 표현" (Discourse/Argumentation Expressions, GRAM-ADV-020~023), the last section of the whole Korean grammar curriculum.

Preview
거의 다 왔으니까 도착한 셈이다. ("We're almost there, so it's basically as if we've arrived.")
매일 책을 조금씩 읽으니까 독서가 취미인 셈이다. ("I read a little every day, so reading basically counts as a hobby of mine.")
도착한 셈이다 (verb 도착하다 + completed -(으)ㄴ 셈이다) rounds off "almost arrived" to "as good as arrived." 취미인 셈이다 (이다 + -인 셈이다) rounds off "reads a little every day" up to "counts as a hobby" — neither sentence claims the literal fact is 100% true, only that it's close enough to call it that.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 The origin: 셈, "a calculation/reckoning"

셈 is a native-Korean bound noun meaning "calculation," "reckoning," or "estimate" — the same root you see in 셈하다 ("to calculate, to count up") and the idiom 셈이 빠르다 ("to be quick with figures," i.e., sharp at sizing up a situation). Grammatically, -(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다 is built exactly like -(으)ㄹ 리가 없다 (GRAM-ADV-016): a modifier attaches to the bound noun 셈, and 이다 ("to be") affirms it. Read piece by piece, 도착한 셈이다 is "[the fact of] having arrived → reckoning/calculation → it is [that reckoning]" — i.e., "if you total it up, it comes out to having arrived." That "doing the math" origin is exactly why the meaning feels evaluative rather than literal: the speaker isn't reporting a bare fact, they're reporting the conclusion of a mental calculation.

Study Tip A handy paraphrase for -(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다 is "practically speaking, ..." or "you could say that...". If your English translation still sounds natural with "basically" or "as good as" inserted in front of it, you've probably found a genuine -(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다 situation.
