-(으)ㄹ 법하다 Conjecture of Plausibility

"That Would Make Sense" — Reasoning Your Way to a Believable Possibility

GRAM-ADV-018 Advanced (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelAdvanced (TOPIK II, 5–6급) — 3rd item of the "추측·전문(傳聞) 고급 표현" (Advanced Conjecture/Hearsay Expressions) cluster, coming right after GRAM-ADV-017 -(으)ㄴ/는 듯하다 (soft observational conjecture) and before GRAM-ADV-019 -(으)ㄴ/는 셈이다
CategoryAdvanced Conjecture/Hearsay Expressions > 4. 추측·전문(傳聞) 고급 표현
Learning Goals ① Understand that -(으)ㄹ 법하다 expresses plausibility — "it would be reasonable/likely that X" or "it wouldn't be surprising if X" — a conjecture reasoned out from the surrounding circumstances, and know that it builds on the bound noun 법 (法, "way/method/reason") + 하다 ("to be so"), literally "there is a plausible way for [X] to be true"
② Correctly form -(으)ㄹ/을 법하다 for verbs and adjectives in the present and past (-았/었을 법하다), use it attributively as -(으)ㄹ 법한 + noun ("a plausible/believable ___"), and use the emphatic variant -(으)ㄹ 법도 하다 ("it would even be plausible that...")
③ Place -(으)ㄹ 법하다 on a three-way spectrum against GRAM-ADV-016 -(으)ㄹ 리가 없다 (confident negative conjecture, near-certainty) and GRAM-ADV-017 -(으)ㄴ/는 듯하다 (soft observational conjecture from outward cues) — -(으)ㄹ 법하다 sits in between, arguing that something is a reasonable, expected possibility given the circumstances, neither confidently denying nor merely describing an impression

1Concept Introduction — When a Possibility "Makes Sense"

GRAM-ADV-017 -(으)ㄴ/는 듯하다 taught you how to report a soft impression from what you can see, hear, or otherwise sense — "it looks/seems like X." -(으)ㄹ 법하다 is a third flavor of advanced conjecture, and it works differently again: instead of reading outward cues, it reasons from the circumstances themselves to argue that a possibility is believable, expected, or simply unsurprising. When you say 그 정도 노력했으면 성공할 법도 하다, you're not reporting an impression and you're not stating a fact — you're reasoning: "given how much effort that took, success would be a perfectly plausible outcome here." It's the grammar of "that would make sense" or "it wouldn't be surprising if."

The key is the dependent noun 법, from the Sino-Korean root 法 meaning "way," "method," or "rule" — the same 법 that appears in 방법 ("method") and 문법 ("grammar," literally "sentence rules"). -(으)ㄹ 법하다 attaches a modifier to 법 and then predicates it with 하다 ("to be so"), so the whole phrase literally reads "there is a plausible way/reason for [X] to be true" — i.e., "X would plausibly happen." This chapter covers the form, the emphatic -(으)ㄹ 법도 하다 variant, and — most importantly — how -(으)ㄹ 법하다 fits between the confident denial of -(으)ㄹ 리가 없다 and the soft impression of -(으)ㄴ/는 듯하다.

Preview
그 정도 노력했으면 성공할 법도 하다. ("Having put in that much effort, it would be quite plausible that he'd succeed.")
이 소설에는 실제로 있을 법한 이야기가 담겨 있다. ("This novel contains a story that could plausibly be real.")
Neither sentence confidently asserts a fact or merely describes an impression — both argue, from the circumstances, that a possibility is reasonable and unsurprising.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 The origin: 법 (法, "way/method/reason") + 하다 ("to be so")

법 is a dependent noun (never used alone) from the Sino-Korean root 法, meaning "way," "method," or "reason" — the same root behind 방법 (方法, "method") and 문법 (文法, "grammar"). Grammatically, -(으)ㄹ 법하다 attaches the modifier -(으)ㄹ to 법 the way it would attach to any noun, and then closes the phrase with 하다 ("to be so, to be the case") rather than a subject-marking particle. Read piece by piece, 갈 법하다 is "[the fact of] going → a plausible way/reason for it → that is so" — i.e., "it would be plausible that [he/she/it] goes."

Study Tip -(으)ㄹ 법하다 belongs to a small family of advanced patterns built the same way: modifier + bound noun + 하다 (compare -(으)ㄴ/는 듯하다, "modifier + 듯 + 하다"). Because 법 combines directly with 하다 rather than with 이다, -(으)ㄹ 법하다 is written and pronounced as one tight unit — don't insert a subject particle (×법이 하다).
