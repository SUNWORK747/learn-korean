-(으)ㄹ망정 A Proud, Defiant Concession

Though It May Be... (I'd Rather)

GRAM-ADV-013 Advanced (TOPIK II 5–6급) 35 min
LevelAdvanced (고급, TOPIK II 5–6급) — 2nd item of the 4-chapter cluster "인과·조건 고급 표현" (Advanced Cause/Condition Expressions), coming right after GRAM-ADV-012 -는 이상
Category인과·조건 고급 표현 (Advanced Cause/Condition Expressions) > 2nd of 4 (GRAM-ADV-012–015). Note: despite living in this "cause/condition" section of the curriculum, -(으)ㄹ망정 is semantically a concessive/contrast pattern — "though/granted that X is true, Y (a proud, defiant stance) still holds." It is closely related to -(으)ㄹ지언정 (GRAM-ADV-023, in the later 담화·논증 표현 section), an even stronger, more literary cousin — we'll compare the two properly once that chapter exists.
Learning Goals ① Understand -(으)ㄹ망정 as meaning "though/granted that [X] is true — a lesser, unfavorable, or humbling condition — [Y] still holds," where Y is a proud, defiant, or morally firm stance the speaker insists on, usually standing in contrast to an implied shameful or dishonorable alternative
② Correctly attach -(으)ㄹ망정 to verb and adjective stems (vowel-final, consonant-final, ㄹ-final, and ㅂ/ㄷ irregulars) and to 이다/아니다, and recognize that it is most natural with adjectives, 이다, and stative predicates that describe a state or condition being conceded
③ Recognize the typical two-part structure of -(으)ㄹ망정 sentences: Clause A (a concession — poverty, hunger, hardship, loss) + -(으)ㄹ망정, Clause B (a strong resolve or values-based refusal, often ending in -겠다 or -지 않다)

1Concept Introduction — Though It May Be... (I'd Rather)

In the curriculum, -(으)ㄹ망정 sits inside "인과·조건 고급 표현" (Advanced Cause/Condition Expressions), right after GRAM-ADV-012 -는 이상. Don't let the section title mislead you, though: -(으)ㄹ망정 has nothing to do with cause-and-effect logic. It is a concessive pattern — one that says, in effect, "granted that this unfavorable thing is true about me, I will still stand by this proud or principled choice." Think of the classic Korean proverb-style sentence 가난할망정 정직하게 살겠다 ("Though I may be poor, I will live honestly") — the first half concedes something humbling (poverty), and the second half plants a flag: no matter that concession, the speaker's dignity or values will not bend.

This gives -(으)ㄹ망정 a distinct emotional color compared to neutral concessives like -아/어도 or even the dramatic -더라도/-(으)ㄹ지라도 you may already know: it isn't just logical ("even if A, B still happens") — it's a value judgment. The speaker is drawing a line: "I accept A about myself, but I refuse to cross into something worse to escape it." Like other 고급 concessives, -(으)ㄹ망정 belongs to 문어체 (the written, literary register) and shows up in proverbs, speeches, letters, and dramatic narration — rarely in everyday chit-chat. It also has a close literary cousin you'll meet later, -(으)ㄹ지언정 (GRAM-ADV-023), which expresses almost the same "I'd rather X than Y" defiance in an even more elevated register.

Preview
가난할망정 정직하게 살겠다. ("Though I may be poor, I will live honestly.")
굶을망정 남의 것은 훔치지 않겠다. ("Even if I starve, I will not steal what belongs to others.")
가난할망정 (가난하다, a vowel-final adjective, + -(으)ㄹ망정) and 굶을망정 (굶다, a consonant-final verb, + -(으)ㄹ망정) both concede a hard, humbling situation — then the second clause draws a proud line that won't be crossed.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: stem + -(으)ㄹ망정

-(으)ㄹ망정 attaches directly to verb and adjective stems, and to 이다/아니다, following the same shape rule as other -(으)ㄹ-family endings you've already met (like -(으)ㄹ지라도): the 으 appears only when the stem ends in a consonant, a vowel-final stem takes -ㄹ망정 directly, and a stem that already ends in ㄹ simply adds -망정 (the extra ㄹ is never doubled). To concede an already-completed or decided situation, -았/었- can be inserted before it, giving -았/었을망정 (e.g. 실패했을망정, "even granting that [I] have already failed") — but this is far less common than the plain form, since -(으)ㄹ망정 most naturally describes an ongoing state or condition rather than a single finished event.

Study Tip 이다 becomes 일망정 for every noun, whether or not it ends in a batchim — 거지일망정 (거지 has no batchim) and 학생일망정 (학생 has a batchim) follow the exact same pattern. And just as with -(으)ㄹ지라도, a ㄹ-final stem never doubles its ㄹ: 살다 becomes 살망정, never 살ㄹ망정. One more thing to notice: -(으)ㄹ망정 leans heavily toward adjectives, 이다, and stative predicates (가난하다, 힘들다, 외롭다, 거지이다) that describe a condition someone is in, rather than plain action verbs describing something someone simply does — the concession is almost always "granted that I am in state X."
