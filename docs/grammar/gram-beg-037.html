-기 전에 / -(으)ㄴ 후에 Before / After

Putting Two Actions in Order

LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — 3rd item of this stage's Essential Expressions section

Learning Goals ① Form -기 전에 ("before doing ~") by attaching it to the plain verb stem with no batchim rule, and remember it never takes a past-tense marker even in a past-tense sentence
② Form -(으)ㄴ 후에 ("after doing ~") using the batchim-dependent 은/ㄴ rule, and understand it inherently marks the first action as already completed

1Concept Introduction — Putting Two Actions in Order

Right after learning obligation (-아/어야 하다, GRAM-BEG-036), the next essential skill is describing the order two actions happen in — "before I eat," "after I finish work." English uses two separate words, "before" and "after," each with its own grammar (they even flip which clause sounds "first" depending on the sentence). Korean instead uses two paired endings that attach directly to a verb stem: -기 전에 ("before ~ing") and -(으)ㄴ 후에 ("after ~ing"). Because they're a matched pair describing opposite sides of the same timeline, this chapter teaches them together.

The two patterns look similar but behave differently: -기 전에 is simple and never changes shape, while -(으)ㄴ 후에 follows the same batchim-dependent (으)ㄴ rule you'll recognize from other "completed action" grammar. Keep them as a pair in your head — one for "before," one for "after" — and the order of clauses in the Korean sentence will start to feel natural.

Preview
저는 자기 전에 이를 닦아요. ("I brush my teeth before going to bed.")
저는 밥을 먹은 후에 커피를 마셔요. ("I drink coffee after eating a meal.")
In both sentences, the action with 전에/후에 comes first in the Korean sentence — the timeline of the actions, not the sentence's word order, is what "before/after" describes.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 -기 전에 ("before ~ing") — no batchim rule, never past tense

-기 전에 attaches to the plain dictionary stem of a verb, exactly the same way every time, no matter what the stem ends in: verb stem + -기 전에. This is the same -기 nominalizer pattern used elsewhere in Korean grammar (it turns the verb into "the ~ing," and 전에 means "before"). Crucially, even when the whole sentence is about the past, the verb inside -기 전에 is never marked with -았/었- — you always say 가기 전에, never 갔기 전에, because logically the "before" action hadn't happened yet at that point.

Verb stem + -기 전에 — the same ending every time, never past tense
Verb (dictionary form)Stem+ -기 전에Meaning
자다 (to sleep)자자기 전에"before sleeping"
먹다 (to eat)먹먹기 전에"before eating"
가다 (to go)가가기 전에"before going"
시작하다 (to start)시작하시작하기 전에"before starting"
Study Tip -기 전에 is one of the easiest patterns in this whole stage: no batchim rule, no irregular verbs to worry about, no past tense to attach. If you know the dictionary form of a verb, you already know its -기 전에 form.
