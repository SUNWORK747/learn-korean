-밖에 Only, and Nothing But

The Particle That Demands a Negative

GRAM-INT-024 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 1st item of the "조사·의존명사 확장" (Particle & Bound Noun Expansion) section, right after the Quotation/Indirect Speech section closes with GRAM-INT-023 -(으)라고 하다
CategoryParticle & Bound Noun Expansion > 5. 조사·의존명사 확장
Learning Goals ① Understand that -밖에 attaches to a noun to mean "nothing but / only," and that it must always be followed by a negative predicate — a -밖에 sentence with no negative is ungrammatical
② Correctly build -밖에 sentences with common negative predicates (안, -지 않다, 못, -지 못하다, 없다, 모르다, 아니다) and read the "double negative" result as a simple positive limit in English
③ Distinguish -밖에 from -만: both can mean "only," but -만 works fine with a positive predicate while -밖에 cannot, and -밖에 usually adds a nuance of insufficiency or regret

1Concept Introduction — "Only," but with a Catch

You already know one way to say "only" in Korean: the particle -만 (GRAM-BEG-008), which you can drop into almost any sentence, positive or negative. -밖에 is Korean's other "only," and it comes with a strict grammatical condition that trips up a lot of learners: the verb or adjective in the sentence must be negative. You can't just swap -밖에 in wherever -만 would go — if the predicate isn't negative, the sentence is simply wrong, not just awkward.

Why would a word meaning "only" need a negative verb? Think of -밖에 literally: it comes from 밖 ("outside") + 에 ("at/in"), so 이것밖에 없어요 literally reads something like "there is nothing outside of this" — a negative statement about everything except the one thing named. That's why English "I only have one" comes out in Korean as a kind of double negative: "I don't have [anything] outside of one." Once you see that logic, the negative-agreement rule stops feeling arbitrary and starts feeling inevitable.

Preview
지갑에 천 원밖에 없어요. ("I only have 1,000 won in my wallet." — literally, "there is nothing outside of 1,000 won.")
저는 영어밖에 못 해요. ("I can only speak English." — literally, "I cannot [speak anything] outside of English.")
In both sentences, 밖에 attaches to a noun (천 원, 영어), and the verb that follows — 없어요 ("there isn't"), 못 해요 ("cannot do") — is negative. Remove the negative and both sentences break.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: noun + 밖에, no exceptions for batchim

-밖에 attaches directly to a noun with a single, unchanging form — unlike many particles, it does not change shape depending on whether the noun ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel. It also replaces the subject particle 이/가 and the object particle 을/를: you don't say 친구가밖에 or 밥을밖에, just 친구밖에 and 밥밖에. It can, however, stack after other particles like 에서, 에게, 한테, and (으)로.

-밖에 attaching to nouns
Noun+ -밖에Meaning
친구 (friend)친구밖에"only a/the friend" (replaces 가)
밥 (rice/meal)밥밖에"only rice/a meal" (replaces 을)
여기 (here)여기밖에"only here"
여기에서 (here, at)여기에서밖에"only [from] here" (stacks after 에서)
Key Rule -밖에 must be followed by a negative predicate somewhere later in the clause: 안/-지 않다, 못/-지 못하다, 없다, 모르다, 아니다, and similar negative expressions. A sentence like *저는 천 원밖에 있어요 is ungrammatical. The correct version is 저는 천 원밖에 없어요. ("I only have 1,000 won.") If you catch yourself writing -밖에 with a plain positive verb, stop and either add a negative or switch to -만.
Common negative predicates that pair with -밖에
Negative formMeaningExample with -밖에
없다"there isn't / don't have"돈이 아니라 시간밖에 없어요.
안 + verb / -지 않다"don't/doesn't"이 노래밖에 안 들어요.
못 + verb / -지 못하다"can't"김치밖에 못 먹어요.
모르다"don't know"이름밖에 몰라요.
아니다"is not"장난이 아니라 진심밖에 없어요.
