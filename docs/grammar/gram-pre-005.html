The 7 Representative Batchim Sounds 받침의 7가지 대표음

How Written Final Consonants Are Actually Pronounced

GRAM-PRE-005 Beginner (Pre-TOPIK) 20 min
LevelBeginner (for students who already know that syllables can end in a 받침, GRAM-PRE-004)
CategoryHangul > 1. Hangul (Alphabet & Pronunciation)
Learning Goals ① State the rule that no matter which consonant letter is written as 받침 (final consonant), only 7 sounds are ever actually pronounced: [ㄱ] [ㄴ] [ㄷ] [ㄹ] [ㅁ] [ㅂ] [ㅇ]
② Map groups of written consonant letters (and common double batchim) to the one representative sound each collapses to in final position
③ Read real words correctly by applying the 7 representative sounds rule

1Concept Introduction — 21 Letters, Only 7 Sounds

In GRAM-PRE-004 you learned that a syllable block can end in a 받침 (final consonant), and that Korean allows a surprisingly large number of consonant letters — and even pairs of consonant letters (겹받침) — to sit in that final slot. If you tried to memorize a separate pronunciation for every single one of those letters, it would feel overwhelming.

Here is the good news: it doesn't matter how many different letters can be written as 받침. When Koreans actually say a word out loud, the final consonant always comes out as one of only 7 representative sounds: [ㄱ] [ㄴ] [ㄷ] [ㄹ] [ㅁ] [ㅂ] [ㅇ]. This is often called batchim neutralization, and it is one of the most useful shortcuts in all of Korean pronunciation — learn these 7 "landing spots" once, and you can read almost any 받침 correctly.

Preview
밖 [박] ("outside") ends in the letter ㄲ, and 부엌 [부억] ("kitchen") ends in the letter ㅋ — two completely different written letters. But listen closely: both words are actually pronounced with the exact same final sound, [ㄱ]. That is batchim neutralization in action.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Why do so many letters collapse into so few sounds?

In final (받침) position, the mouth and tongue can only come to a complete stop in 7 distinct ways — so many consonant letters that sound clearly different at the start of a syllable end up sounding identical at the end of one. You do not need to master the linguistic theory behind this — you just need to memorize the 7 landing sounds and which written letters land on each one.
