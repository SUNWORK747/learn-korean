이/가 Subject Particle

Marking the Grammatical Subject — New Information

GRAM-BEG-002 Elementary (TOPIK I) 20 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — second grammar item of this stage
CategoryParticles > 1. Particles
Learning Goals ① Attach 이/가 correctly based on whether the preceding noun ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel — 이 after a consonant, 가 after a vowel
② Use 이/가 to mark the grammatical subject, especially for NEW information, contrasting with 은/는's topic/known-information nuance (GRAM-BEG-001)
③ Recognize the irregular contracted subject forms for "I": 나+가 → 내가 (not "나가"), 저+가 → 제가

1Concept Introduction — The Other Subject-Like Particle

In GRAM-BEG-001 you learned 은/는, which marks the topic of a sentence — "as for X, ...". Now you'll learn 이/가, the particle that marks the plain grammatical subject of a sentence: the noun that is doing the action or being described. Both particles can attach to the same noun in different sentences, but they signal different things to the listener — which one you choose changes the nuance of the sentence.

이/가 tends to appear when the information is new to the conversation, or when the sentence is directly answering a "who?" or "what?" question. Think of it as pointing at the subject and saying "this one, specifically" — rather than 은/는's "as for this one (which we're already discussing)."

Preview
책이 있어요 (chaegi isseoyo, "there is a book") and 친구가 와요 (chinguga wayo, "a friend is coming") both use 이/가 to mark the subject — but notice one attaches 이 and the other 가. The rule behind that difference is the first thing you'll learn in this chapter.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Choosing 이 vs. 가 — the batchim rule

이/가 attaches directly to the end of a noun, with no space in writing. Which form you use depends only on whether the noun ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel — the same batchim logic you already used for 은/는 in GRAM-BEG-001 and for 이에요/예요 in GRAM-PRE-017.

이 vs. 가 — attachment rule
Noun endingParticleExampleMeaning
Consonant (받침 있음)이책이 있어요"there is a book"
Vowel (받침 없음)가친구가 와요"a friend is coming"
Consonant (받침 있음)이사람이 있어요"there is a person / someone is there"
Vowel (받침 없음)가학교가 커요"the school is big"
Study Tip Same pattern, new particle: consonant-ending noun → the form starting with a consonant (이); vowel-ending noun → the form starting with a vowel (가). This is the identical logic from 은/는 (GRAM-BEG-001) and 이에요/예요 (GRAM-PRE-017) — only the particle itself changes, not the rule for choosing between its two forms.
