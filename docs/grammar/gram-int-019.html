1Concept Introduction — Making Someone Do Something

In GRAM-INT-018 you learned the passive (피동) — how to say something "gets done" to the subject, with the agent pushed into the background. The causative (사동) is the mirror image: it lets you say that the subject makes, has, or lets someone else do something. Instead of "the child eats," you say "mom feeds the child" (literally, "mom makes the child eat"). Korean marks this with either a small set of irregular suffixes attached directly to the verb, or with the general-purpose pattern -게 하다 added after the verb stem.

There are two systems to learn here, and they work very differently. The irregular suffixes —-이-, -히-, -리-, -기-, -우-, -구-, -추-— only attach to a specific, closed list of verbs that must be memorized one by one (there's no rule that predicts which suffix a given verb takes). The pattern -게 하다, on the other hand, is fully productive: it can attach after almost any verb stem, and it's the only option for verbs that have no irregular causative form. Because several of these same suffixes (-이/히/리/기-) are also used to form the passive you just studied, this chapter ends with a close comparison of the two so you can tell them apart.

Preview
엄마가 아기에게 밥을 먹여요. ("Mom feeds the baby." — literally, "Mom makes the baby eat.")
선생님이 학생들에게 교실을 청소하게 했어요. ("The teacher made the students clean the classroom.")
먹여요 (먹다 + irregular suffix -이-) is a memorized causative form. 청소하게 했어요 (청소하다 + -게 하다) uses the productive pattern, since 청소하다 has no irregular causative suffix. Both sentences add a new subject — the causer — who makes someone else act.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 What is 사동 (causative)? Adding a "causer"

A basic sentence like 아이가 밥을 먹어요 ("The child eats.") has one participant: the child, who is both the subject and the one doing the eating. A causative sentence adds a second participant — the causer — who becomes the new subject, while the original subject (the child) is demoted to the person being caused to act, usually marked with 에게/한테 ("to/for"). The object, if there is one, stays as the object.

Argument structure: base sentence vs. causative sentence
Sentence typeSubject (이/가)CauseeObject (을/를)Verb
Base아이가 (the child)—밥을 (rice/food)먹어요 (eats)
Causative엄마가 (mom, the causer)아이에게 (to the child)밥을 (rice/food)먹여요 (feeds / makes eat)
