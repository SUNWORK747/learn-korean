-고 Connective: Listing ("And")

Joining Two Actions or Qualities — Opening the Connective Endings Section

GRAM-BEG-023 Elementary (TOPIK I) 20 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — first item of this stage's Connective Endings section
CategoryConnective Endings > 3. Connective Endings (연결어미)
Learning Goals ① Attach -고 directly to any verb or adjective stem (no batchim distinction, no vowel-harmony rule) to connect two clauses and mean "and"
② Recognize that -고 lists actions or qualities that happen in sequence or simply together, without implying a strict cause-and-effect relationship — and that the two clauses can have different subjects
③ Know that tense markers (-았/었-, -겠-) are normally attached only once, on the final clause, not on the clause before -고

1Concept Introduction — Joining Sentences Together

So far you've learned to build single, complete sentences: subject, object, verb, all wrapped up with an ending like -아/어요. But real speech constantly joins ideas together — "I eat breakfast and go to school," "This dress is pretty and cheap." -고 is the simplest tool for that job: a connective ending that links two clauses into one sentence, most often translating as "and."

This chapter opens a brand-new section of the syllabus called "Connective Endings (연결어미)." Over the next few chapters (GRAM-BEG-024 -지만, GRAM-BEG-025 -아/어서, GRAM-BEG-026 -(으)니까, and beyond) you'll learn connectives that express contrast, reason, and condition. -고 is the most neutral of them all — it simply lists things together — which makes it the natural starting point.

Preview
저는 아침을 먹고 학교에 가요. ("I eat breakfast and go to school.")
이 옷은 예쁘고 싸요. ("This dress is pretty and cheap.")
The first sentence lists two actions done in order by the same person; the second lists two qualities of the same dress. Both use the same unchanging -고.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Attaching -고 — no batchim rule needed

-고 attaches directly to a verb or adjective stem: stem + -고. Just like -고 싶다 from the last chapter, it never changes depending on whether the stem ends in a consonant (batchim) or a vowel, and there's no vowel-harmony rule to check.

Stem + -고 — always the same shape
Word (dictionary form)Stem+ -고Meaning
가다 (to go)가가고"go, and..."
먹다 (to eat)먹먹고"eat, and..."
좋다 (to be good)좋좋고"be good, and..."
있다 (to exist/have)있있고"have, and..."
Study Tip Compare with -아/어요 (GRAM-BEG-013), which needs you to check vowel harmony every time. -고 is much easier: whatever the stem looks like, you just add 고. That's why it's the first connective you learn.
