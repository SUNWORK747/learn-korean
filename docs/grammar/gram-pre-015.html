Statement · Question · Command · Suggestion

LevelBeginner (for students who already know Hangul and can read simple syllables)

Learning Goals ① Name the 4 sentence types by communicative function: statement (평서문), question (의문문), command (명령문), and suggestion/"let's" (청유문)

1Concept Introduction — Four Jobs a Sentence Can Do

Every sentence you speak is doing one of a small number of "jobs": telling someone something, asking them something, telling them to do something, or proposing that you do something together. English marks these jobs with a mix of word order, helper verbs, and punctuation — think of how "You eat rice" becomes "Do you eat rice?" by moving a word to the front. Korean marks the exact same four jobs in a completely different way: by changing (or keeping) the sentence-final ending, backed up by intonation in speech and punctuation in writing.

In this chapter you will meet the 4 basic sentence types by name — statement (평서문), question (의문문), command (명령문), and suggestion / "let's" (청유문) — and learn how to tell them apart on the page, using the same simple verb 가다 ("to go") throughout so you can compare all four side by side.

Preview
학교에 가요. (Statement — "I go to school.")
학교에 가요? (Question — "Are you going to school?")
학교에 가세요. (Command — "Please go to school.")
학교에 갑시다. (Suggestion — "Let's go to school.")

2Concept Explanation

2.1 The 4 sentence types, by function

Linguists sort sentences by what they are used to do, not just by their grammar. Korean grammar books traditionally name four core types (a fifth, the exclamatory sentence 감탄문, exists too but is far less common at the beginner level and is not covered here).

The 4 sentence types by communicative function
TypeKorean termFunctionEnglish example
Statement평서문Declares a fact or opinion"I eat rice."
Question의문문Asks for information or a yes/no answer"Do you eat rice?"
Command명령문Orders or requests someone to act"Eat rice."
Suggestion ("let's")청유문Proposes doing something together"Let's eat rice."

3Examples

3.1 One verb, four sentence types — 가다 ("to go")

Compare all four sentence types built from the same verb, so only the ending (and punctuation) changes.

가다 ("to go") in all 4 sentence types
TypeKoreanEnglishEnding used
Statement학교에 가요.I go to school.-아/어요 + .
Question학교에 가요?Are you going to school?-아/어요 + ?
Command학교에 가세요.Please go to school.-세요
Suggestion학교에 갑시다.Let's go to school.-읍시다/-ㅂ시다
Look closely
학교에 가요. and 학교에 가요? are written with the identical ending -아/어요 — only the final punctuation mark (and, in speech, the rising pitch) tells you it's a question and not a statement.
