-조차/마저 Extreme Inclusion ("Even")

When Even the Unexpected Case Is Included

GRAM-INT-025 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 2nd item of the "조사·의존명사 확장" (Particles & Bound Nouns Expansion) section, right after GRAM-INT-024 -밖에
CategoryParticles & Bound Nouns Expansion > 5. 조사·의존명사 확장
Learning Goals ① Understand that -조차 and -마저 both mean "even," attaching directly to a noun to add extra emphasis to an already surprising or extreme case
② Distinguish the nuance: -조차 highlights the single most extreme or least-expected case, almost always in a negative sentence, while -마저 highlights the last remaining item in a series (usually negative, but occasionally neutral or positive)
③ Compare -조차/-마저 with the basic inclusive particle 도 ("also/even"), and see how they connect to -밖에 (GRAM-INT-024) as another negative-leaning restrictive/emphatic particle

1Concept Introduction — Adding "Even" to an Already Extreme Case

This item follows directly from GRAM-INT-024 -밖에 (only, restriction with a negative verb), and the two belong together in spirit. Where -밖에 says "nothing except X" (limiting a sentence to a single item), -조차 and -마저 both mean "even" — they take a noun that is already an extreme, unlikely, or last-remaining case, and mark it as included in a statement that is usually negative or unfortunate. Both particles attach directly to a noun (no batchim distinction, unlike 이/가 or 을/를), and both replace 을/를 when they appear, the same way -밖에, 도, and 만 do.

The two aren't interchangeable, though they overlap heavily. -조차 tends to spotlight the single most basic or least expected case failing — "I don't even know his name" implies that even the easiest fact to know is missing. -마저 tends to spotlight the last one left in a series — "even he left" implies other people already left, and now the very last one is gone too. Both are usually negative in tone, but -마저 can occasionally show up in a neutral or even mildly positive "finishing touch" context, which -조차 almost never does. This chapter walks through the form, the nuance split, and how both compare to the everyday particle 도 ("also/even").

Preview
이름조차 몰라요. ("I don't even know [his/her] name.")
그 사람마저 떠났어요. ("Even that person — the last one who was still here — left.")
이름조차 marks "name" as the most basic fact that should be easy to know, yet even that fails. 사람마저 marks "that person" as the last one remaining in a group that has already been leaving — and now they, too, are gone.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: attaching -조차/-마저 to a noun

Unlike verb/adjective endings, -조차 and -마저 are particles, so the form never changes for tense or final consonant (batchim) — the noun simply takes -조차 or -마저 directly, and either replaces 을/를 or is added after other particles such as 에서, 한테, 까지.

-조차/-마저 attachment patterns
SituationPatternExample
Plain noun (subject/object position)Noun + 조차/마저이름조차, 친구마저
Replacing 을/를Noun + 조차/마저 (을/를 dropped)밥을 먹다 → 밥조차 못 먹다
Stacking after other particlesNoun + 에서/한테/까지 + 조차/마저여기서조차, 가족한테마저
No batchim-based change조차/마저 (always the same shape)물조차, 밥조차, 친구마저, 사람마저
Study Tip Both -조차 and -마저 pair overwhelmingly with negative predicates (몰라요, 못 해요, 없어요, 떠났어요, etc.), just like -밖에 (GRAM-INT-024) requires a negative verb. Think of all three as part of the same family of "negative-leaning" particles — they restrict or intensify meaning in sentences that describe something missing, failing, or lost.
