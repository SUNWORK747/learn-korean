-는바 Presenting a Situation, Formally

The Written, Bureaucratic Cousin of -는데

1Concept Introduction — Setting Up an Official Statement

This is the 5th item of Advanced (고급, TOPIK II 5–6급) Korean, and it belongs to the 문어체 (muneoche), or "written register" — grammar you will meet constantly in essays, news articles, official announcements, business reports, and legal documents, but almost never in casual conversation. -는바 is one of the most recognizable markers of this register: it is a formal connective that takes a fact or circumstance, presents it as background, and then leads into a related statement — typically a decision, an announcement, or a polite but official request. You will see it constantly in workplace emails, public notices, meeting minutes, and legal writing.

Structurally, -는바 attaches to a verb stem the same way the familiar -는데 (GRAM-INT-001) does, and the two endings are historically and functionally related. Think of -는바 as -는데's formal, bureaucratic cousin: it does the same job of "setting the scene" before a related comment, but it is restricted to written, official contexts, it almost always leads into a formal directive or announcement, and — unlike -는데 — it never carries the "but" / mild-contrast meaning. This chapter covers the form, the core function, and a direct comparison to -는데 so you can transfer what you already know.

Preview
회의 결과를 아래와 같이 알려 드리는바, 참고하시기 바랍니다. ("We hereby inform you of the meeting results as follows; please take note.")
본 제품에서 하자가 발견되었는바, 전량 회수하기로 결정하였습니다. ("A defect has been found in this product, and it has been decided to recall the entire quantity.")
알려 드리는바 (알리다 + -는바) presents "informing you of the results" as the background, then leads into the formal request 참고하시기 바랍니다 ("please take note"). 발견되었는바 (past tense + -는바) presents the discovered defect as the circumstance, then leads into the official decision that follows. Same pattern every time: situation first, official statement second.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: mostly verbs, with rarer adjective/이다 forms

-는바 attaches directly to a verb stem (and to 있다/없다, which conjugate like verbs) in its ordinary, present/general form. For a completed situation, the past tense marker -았/었- simply slots in before -는바, giving -았/었는바 — the 는바 part itself does not change. Adjectives and 이다 can take -는바's close relatives -(으)ㄴ바/인바, but this is far less common and appears mainly in legal or highly bureaucratic documents (판결문, 진술서 등); in everyday formal writing, -는바 overwhelmingly appears on verbs.

-는바/-(으)ㄴ바/인바 by word type and tense
Word typeFormExample
Verb (동사), present/general-는바알리다 → 알리는바
Verb (동사), past/completed-았/었는바발견하다 → 발견되었는바
있다/없다-는바없다 → 없는바
Adjective (형용사) — rare, legal/formal-(으)ㄴ바부족하다 → 부족한바
이다 — rare, legal/formal인바무관한 자이다 → 무관한 자인바
Study Tip Because -는바 is a purely written, formal connective, it is almost never taught as a "conjugate this adjective/verb" drill the way -는데 is. Focus your energy on recognizing and producing the verb form (V-는바 / V-았/었는바) — that covers the vast majority of real usage in notices, reports, and business writing. Treat -(으)ㄴ바/인바 as "good to recognize" rather than "must produce."
