-(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 Almost Happened, But Didn't

The Near-Miss Pattern, and 하마터면

GRAM-INT-017 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 25 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — the 6th and final item of the "추측·의지 표현" (Assumption & Intention Expressions) section, following GRAM-INT-016 -기로 하다
CategoryAssumption & Intention Expressions > 2. 추측·의지 표현
Learning Goals ① Understand that -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 describes an event that almost happened but ultimately did NOT happen, and that it is essentially always used in the past tense, -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다
② Correctly attach -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 to verb stems: -ㄹ 뻔하다 after a vowel or ㄹ-final stem, -을 뻔하다 after a consonant-final stem
③ Use the common collocation 하마터면 … -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다 to emphasize how close a near-miss was, and distinguish -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 from -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 (GRAM-INT-012)

1Concept Introduction — A Close Call That Never Actually Happened

Imagine you're running for the bus, it starts to pull away, and you just barely catch it — or you trip on the stairs but grab the railing just in time. In English, you'd say "I almost missed the bus" or "I almost fell." -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 is exactly that pattern in Korean: it attaches to a verb to describe an event that came very close to happening, but in the end did not happen. The danger, the accident, the mistake — it was avoided, narrowly.

Because -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 always describes something that is already over and safely didn't happen, it is almost always used in the past tense: -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다. You'll rarely if ever see it in present or future tense on its own. This chapter also introduces its favorite partner-in-crime, the adverb 하마터면 ("almost," "by a hair"), which shows up at the start of the sentence to underline just how close the call really was.

Preview
하마터면 늦을 뻔했어요. ("I almost was late." — but I wasn't.)
넘어질 뻔했어요. ("I almost fell down." — but I caught myself.)
Both sentences describe a bad outcome that was avoided at the last moment — the speaker was NOT late, and did NOT fall. That "it didn't actually happen" meaning is the whole point of -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: attaching -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 to the verb stem

-(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 follows the same "ㄹ vs. 을" pattern you've already seen in -(으)ㄹ 거예요, -(으)ㄹ 수 있다/없다, and -(으)ㄹ 것 같다: a stem ending in a vowel (or in the consonant ㄹ) takes -ㄹ 뻔하다, while a stem ending in any other consonant (a batchim) takes -을 뻔하다. Since the event is already finished and safely avoided, 뻔하다 itself is almost always conjugated to the past tense --> 뻔했다/뻔했어요.

-(으)ㄹ 뻔하다: stem type → form → example
Stem typeAttached formDictionary formPast tense (the form you'll actually use)
Vowel-final stem-ㄹ 뻔하다넘어지다 → 넘어질 뻔하다넘어질 뻔했어요
ㄹ-final stem-ㄹ 뻔하다 (no extra ㄹ added)살다 → 살 뻔하다살 뻔했어요*
Consonant-final stem-을 뻔하다늦다 → 늦을 뻔하다늦을 뻔했어요

* ㄹ-final stems are rare with this pattern in everyday speech; the vowel-stem and consonant-stem rows above cover almost every example you'll actually use.

Study Tip -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 attaches to action verbs (동사) that describe an event — falling, missing something, having an accident, forgetting — not to descriptive adjectives (형용사). That makes sense once you remember the meaning: only an event can "almost happen."

3Examples

3.1 -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 in real sentences

KoreanMeaningWhat actually happened
하마터면 늦을 뻔했어요."I almost was late."The speaker was on time.
계단에서 넘어질 뻔했어요."I almost fell on the stairs."The speaker did not fall.
하마터면 사고가 날 뻔했어요."There was almost an accident."No accident occurred.
지갑을 잃어버릴 뻔했어요."I almost lost my wallet."The wallet was not lost.
약속을 잊을 뻔했어요."I almost forgot the appointment."The speaker remembered in time.
