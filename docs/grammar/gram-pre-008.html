Nasalization 비음화

Basic Pronunciation Rules — When Batchim Sounds Turn Nasal

GRAM-PRE-008 Beginner (Pre-TOPIK) 20 min
LevelBeginner (for students with no prior knowledge of Hangul)
CategoryHangul > 1. Hangul (Alphabet & Pronunciation)
Learning Goals ① State the nasalization rule: the representative batchim sounds [ㄱ] [ㄷ] [ㅂ] become the nasal sounds [ㅇ] [ㄴ] [ㅁ] respectively when the following syllable begins with a nasal consonant (ㄴ or ㅁ)
② Apply the rule to read real words correctly, using the 7-representative-sound mapping from GRAM-PRE-005 as the starting point
③ Recognize that nasalization applies after batchim neutralization — first find the representative sound of the written batchim, then check whether nasalization applies

1Concept Introduction — A Rule Built on Top of GRAM-PRE-005

In GRAM-PRE-005 you learned that every written batchim, no matter which of the 19 final consonants it is, actually gets pronounced as one of only 7 representative sounds: [ㄱ], [ㄴ], [ㄷ], [ㄹ], [ㅁ], [ㅂ], or [ㅇ]. That chapter stopped there — treating each syllable as if it were alone. But real Korean words are strings of syllables, and the sound right after a batchim can push that batchim to change again. Nasalization (비음화) is the first, and most common, of these "chain reaction" pronunciation rules.

The rule is simple once you know your 7 representative sounds: whenever a syllable ending in the representative sound [ㄱ], [ㄷ], or [ㅂ] is immediately followed by a syllable that starts with a nasal consonant (ㄴ or ㅁ), that batchim itself turns into the matching nasal sound — [ㄱ]→[ㅇ], [ㄷ]→[ㄴ], [ㅂ]→[ㅁ]. Your mouth is simply "borrowing" the airflow of the upcoming nasal sound one syllable early.

Preview
국물 (guk-mul, "broth") looks like it should be read "guk-mul," but no Korean speaker actually says it that way. The batchim ㄱ sits right before a syllable starting with ㅁ, so it nasalizes into ㅇ, and the word is pronounced [궁물].

2.1 Recap — The 7 Representative Batchim Sounds (GRAM-PRE-005)

Before applying nasalization, you must already know which representative sound a written batchim reduces to. Here is the mapping from GRAM-PRE-005 as a quick reference.

The 7 representative batchim sounds
Representative SoundWritten Batchim Letters That Reduce To It
[ㄱ]ㄱ, ㅋ, ㄲ, ㄳ, ㄺ
[ㄴ]ㄴ, ㄵ, ㄶ
[ㄷ]ㄷ, ㅌ, ㅅ, ㅆ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅎ
[ㄹ]ㄹ, ㄼ, ㄽ, ㄾ, ㅀ
[ㅁ]ㅁ, ㄻ
[ㅂ]ㅂ, ㅍ, ㄿ, ㅄ
[ㅇ]ㅇ
Study Tip Only 3 of these 7 representative sounds can ever nasalize: [ㄱ], [ㄷ], and [ㅂ]. [ㄴ], [ㅁ], and [ㅇ] are already nasal or already match, and [ㄹ] follows a different rule entirely (covered in a later chapter). So nasalization only ever affects words ending in a representative stop sound — [ㄱ]/[ㄷ]/[ㅂ].
