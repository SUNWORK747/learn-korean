-는 대로 Immediate Sequence & Exactly As

The Moment It Happens, or Exactly the Way It Was

③ Distinguish -는 대로 from -자마자 (GRAM-INT-004): both mean "as soon as," but -는 대로 adds a sense of "immediately, without delay or change" and usually leads into an instruction-like action, while -자마자 is a more purely temporal connector that works with any second clause

1Concept Introduction — The Moment It Happens, or Exactly the Way It Was

-는 대로 is a small but very useful pattern built on the bound noun 대로, and it actually does two related jobs. Attached to a verb in its present-tense form, V-는 대로 means "as soon as" — it tells your listener that the moment one action is finished, another action (usually a plan, a promise, or a request) will follow right away: 도착하는 대로 전화할게요. ("I'll call you as soon as I arrive.") Attached instead to a completed action, or straight onto a noun, V-(으)ㄴ 대로 / Noun+대로 means "exactly as" or "according to" — it says that something was done in precisely the same way as an instruction, an expectation, or an example, with no changes: 제가 말한 대로 하세요. ("Do exactly as I told you.")

These two uses share the same core idea — 대로 always signals "matching something exactly, without deviation," whether that "something" is a point in time (as soon as it happens) or a manner (the way it was said, planned, or expected). Once you see the shared logic, the two uses stop feeling like separate grammar points and start feeling like one flexible tool. This chapter covers both uses side by side, and then compares -는 대로 with the more familiar -자마자 (GRAM-INT-004), since both can be translated "as soon as" but they are not always interchangeable.

Preview
수업이 끝나는 대로 연락드릴게요. ("I'll contact you as soon as class ends.")
제가 시킨 대로 순서대로 정리해 주세요. ("Please organize it in order, exactly as I told you.")
끝나는 대로 (present stem 끝나 + -는 대로) marks a moment in time — the instant class ends. 시킨 대로 (past stem 시키 + -(으)ㄴ 대로) and 순서대로 (noun 순서 "order" + 대로) both mark a manner — doing something exactly the way it was instructed or exactly following a sequence.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: three shapes of 대로

대로 attaches in three different shapes depending on what comes before it. For the "as soon as" meaning, it is always present-tense verb stem + -는 대로, regardless of the overall sentence's real-world tense. For the "exactly as" meaning, it is either past/completed verb stem + -(으)ㄴ 대로 (batchim → -은 대로, no batchim → -ㄴ 대로), or, even more simply, a noun directly followed by 대로 with no space (예상대로, 계획대로, 마음대로).

-는 대로 / -(으)ㄴ 대로 / Noun+대로 by form
FormAttaches toMeaningExample
V-는 대로Present verb stem"as soon as"도착하는 대로 (arrive → as soon as I arrive)
V-(으)ㄴ 대로Past/completed verb stem"exactly as (it happened/was done)"말한 대로 (say → exactly as [I/you] said)
Noun+대로Noun, no space"according to / as N"예상대로 (expectation → as expected)
Study Tip Don't let the shared "-는 대로" spelling confuse the two verb uses: with an action verb like 도착하다 or 끝나다, -는 대로 almost always means "as soon as" (a moment in time). With a verb of thinking, wanting, or knowing — like 원하다 ("to want") or 아는 (from 알다, "to know") — -는 대로 instead describes a manner: 원하는 대로 하세요. ("Do as you wish.") 아는 대로 말해 주세요. ("Please tell me everything you know," literally "as you know it.") Context and the verb's meaning tell you which reading is intended.
