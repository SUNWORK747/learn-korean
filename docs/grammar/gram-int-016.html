-기로 하다 Firm Decisions & Promises

Making Up Your Mind, and Keeping a Promise

1Concept Introduction — Making Up Your Mind, and Keeping a Promise

In the last chapter, GRAM-INT-015 introduced -(으)려고 하다, which describes a plan or intention that's still somewhat loose — something you're thinking about doing, or about to do, but that could still change. -기로 하다 is the next step up in commitment: it marks a decision that has actually been made, whether that decision is a personal resolution ("I've decided to…") or an agreement made with someone else ("We've agreed to…", "I promised to…"). Once you say -기로 했어요, the matter is settled — you're not weighing options anymore, you've chosen.

-기로 하다 attaches directly to a verb stem (no batchim rules to worry about, unlike -는데 or -(으)려고 하다) and is built from two pieces: -기 (a noun-like ending that turns the verb into "the act of…") plus 로 (a particle meaning roughly "as/to") plus 하다 ("to do"). Literally, "저는 운동하기로 했어요" is close to "I did [the choosing] as/toward exercising" — which is why the decision itself is almost always described in the past tense, even when the exercising hasn't happened yet.

Preview
저는 내년에 한국어를 배우기로 했어요. ("I've decided to learn Korean next year.")
우리는 다음 주에 만나기로 했어요. ("We agreed to meet next week.")
배우기로 했어요 marks a personal decision that's already final, even though the learning itself starts next year. 만나기로 했어요 marks a shared promise between two people. In both sentences, 했어요 (past tense) tells you the deciding/promising happened already — not the action itself.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: verb stem + -기로 하다

-기로 하다 is refreshingly simple to form: take any verb stem and attach -기로 하다 directly — there's no present/past split in the ending itself and no batchim (final consonant) rule to check, unlike many other intermediate patterns. The tense is carried entirely by 하다, which conjugates normally (하다 → 해요/합니다/했어요/했습니다).

Verb stem + -기로 하다
VerbStem+ -기로 하다Meaning
가다 (to go)가-가기로 하다to decide to go
먹다 (to eat)먹-먹기로 하다to decide to eat
운동하다 (to exercise)운동하-운동하기로 하다to decide to exercise
만나다 (to meet)만나-만나기로 하다to agree/promise to meet
Study Tip -기로 하다 attaches to action verbs (동사), not descriptive adjectives — you can decide to do something, but you can't really "decide to be pretty." If you want to talk about deciding to bring about a state or change, use the -아/어지다 (become) form of the adjective as the base verb instead, e.g. 건강해지기로 했어요 ("I decided to become healthy").
