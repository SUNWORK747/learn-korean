Linking (Liaison) 연음

Basic Pronunciation Rules — How Batchim Sounds Move to the Next Syllable

GRAM-PRE-007 Beginner (Pre-TOPIK) 20 min
LevelBeginner (for students who already know the 7 representative batchim sounds from GRAM-PRE-005)
CategoryHangul > 1. Hangul (Alphabet & Pronunciation)
Learning Goals ① State the linking rule: when a syllable ending in a batchim is followed by a syllable that starts with the silent placeholder ㅇ (a vowel-initial syllable), the batchim consonant sound moves to become the initial sound of the next syllable
② Apply the linking rule to read real words and short phrases correctly
③ Recognize the exception: when the batchim is ㅇ [ng] itself, it does not "move" the same way other consonants do, since ㅇ as a final represents a nasal sound rather than a sound that can relocate

1Concept Introduction — Why Written Syllables Don't Always Sound Separate

In GRAM-PRE-005 you learned that a batchim is always pronounced as one of 7 representative sounds when it sits at the end of a word or is followed by another consonant. But Korean syllables are rarely spoken in isolation — they are constantly followed by other syllables, and when the very next syllable happens to start with a vowel (written with the silent placeholder ㅇ in the initial position), something different happens: instead of staying put, the batchim consonant jumps over and becomes the initial sound of that next syllable. This process is called linking, or liaison (연음).

Linking happens constantly in real spoken Korean, most often when a grammatical particle such as 이/가, 을/를, 은/는, or 에 attaches to a noun that ends in a batchim. Because this happens in nearly every sentence you will ever speak or hear, mastering this one rule is one of the fastest ways to make your pronunciation sound natural instead of choppy.

Preview
옷 (ot, "clothes") plus the subject particle 이 is written 옷이 — but no Korean speaker pronounces this as two separate blocks "ot" + "i." Instead, the batchim ㅅ moves over onto 이, and the whole word is pronounced as one smooth syllable pair: [오시] (o-si).

2Concept Explanation

2.1 The linking rule (formula)

The rule applies whenever a batchim-ending syllable is immediately followed by a syllable built from the silent placeholder ㅇ plus a vowel. In that situation, the batchim consonant "relocates" to become the new initial consonant of the following syllable:

In words: [Syllable 1 = Consonant + Vowel + Batchim] + [Syllable 2 = ㅇ + Vowel] is pronounced as [Syllable 1 without its batchim] + [Batchim consonant as the new initial of Syllable 2 + its vowel]. The batchim does not disappear — it simply moves one syllable to the right.
