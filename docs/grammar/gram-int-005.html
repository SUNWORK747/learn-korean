Something Happening for as Long as Something Else Does

1Concept Introduction — Something Happening for as Long as Something Else Does

You just learned -자마자 (GRAM-INT-004), which links two actions that happen back to back, one instant right after the other. -는 동안 is almost the opposite idea: instead of a single moment, it describes an entire stretch of time during which something else is going on. The word 동안 is actually a noun on its own, meaning "duration" or "period" — so -는 동안 literally builds the phrase "the period during which [verb] is happening," and then something else is said to happen inside that period.

This makes -는 동안 useful for two closely related jobs: describing an action that overlaps with another action for its whole length ("while I was sleeping, my sister cleaned my room"), and, without any verb at all, simply naming a block of time using a duration noun ("for three years," "during summer vacation"). Because 동안 is a noun underneath it all, both patterns share the same word and the same core meaning of "span of time" — this chapter covers both.

Preview
제가 자는 동안 동생이 제 방을 청소했어요. ("While I was sleeping, my younger sibling cleaned my room.")
방학 동안 저는 한국어를 열심히 공부했어요. ("During vacation, I studied Korean hard.")
자는 동안 (자다 + -는 동안) shows the verb pattern — two different people, one action lasting through the other's action. 방학 동안 shows the simpler noun pattern — 동안 attached directly to a time-period noun, no verb needed.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: verb + -는 동안, or a duration noun + 동안

For verbs, attach -는 동안 directly to the stem — the same present-tense modifier shape used in -는 것 (GRAM-BEG-038) and -는데 (GRAM-INT-001). 있다/없다 follow the same rule and take -는 동안, just like verbs. For a plain span of time, skip the verb entirely and attach 동안 straight onto a noun that already names a period or length of time, such as a number of hours or a season.

-는 동안 (verbs) vs. Noun + 동안 (durations)
PatternAttaches toExampleMeaning
Verb (동사) + -는 동안Verb stem기다리다 → 기다리는 동안"while waiting"
있다/없다 + -는 동안있다/없다 stem없다 → 없는 동안"while (someone) is out/absent"
Adjective (형용사) + -(으)ㄴ 동안Adjective stem젊다 → 젊은 동안"while (one is) young" — less common, but exists
Noun (시간/기간) + 동안Duration noun directly방학 동안, 두 시간 동안"during vacation," "for two hours"
Study Tip -는 동안 almost always pairs with action verbs (동사), because 동안 needs an ongoing action to measure a span against. The adjective form -(으)ㄴ 동안 exists (e.g. 젊은 동안, "while young") but is far less common — at this level, focus on mastering the verb pattern -는 동안 and the noun pattern Noun + 동안 first.
