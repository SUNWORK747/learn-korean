1Concept Introduction — A Second, More Formal Way to End a Sentence

In GRAM-BEG-013 you learned -아/어요, the friendly, everyday ending known as 해요체. Korean, however, has more than one "polite" way to end a sentence — and this chapter introduces the second one: -ㅂ니다/습니다, called 합쇼체 (the "formal style"). Both endings are grammatically polite (you're not being rude either way), but they belong to different registers — different levels of social distance and formality.

Think of the difference the way you might think of "Hi, how's it going?" versus "Good evening, thank you for joining us" in English: same basic politeness, very different occasion. -ㅂ니다/습니다 is the ending you'll hear from news anchors, hear in formal presentations, and use yourself in job interviews, military settings, or customer-service speech — anywhere the situation calls for extra distance and formality.

Preview
갑니다 (gamnida, "[I/we] go") and 먹습니다 (meokseumnida, "[I/we] eat") are the 합쇼체 counterparts of the 해요체 forms 가요 and 먹어요 you already know. Same meaning, more formal register. The rule for which one attaches — -ㅂ니다 or -습니다 — is the first thing you'll learn in this chapter.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Choosing -ㅂ니다 vs. -습니다 — the batchim rule

Just like many endings before it, the choice between -ㅂ니다 and -습니다 depends on whether the verb/adjective stem ends in a vowel or a consonant (받침). Drop -다 from the dictionary form to find the stem, then attach the matching ending.

-ㅂ니다 vs. -습니다 — attachment rule
Stem endingEndingDictionary formStemFormal style
Vowel (받침 없음)-ㅂ니다가다 (to go)가갑니다
Consonant (받침 있음)-습니다먹다 (to eat)먹먹습니다
Vowel (받침 없음)-ㅂ니다오다 (to come)오옵니다
Consonant (받침 있음)-습니다읽다 (to read)읽읽습니다
Key Rule Vowel-ending stem → add -ㅂ니다 directly (it merges into the syllable block as a final consonant). Consonant-ending stem → add -습니다 as its own extra syllable. A special case: 이다 ("to be") becomes 입니다 — the formal counterpart of 이에요/예요 from GRAM-PRE-017. For example, 학생입니다 ("[I] am a student") is the formal version of 학생이에요.
