-(으)ㄹ수록 The More... The More

Expressing Proportional Change

GRAM-INT-008 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 25 min

Learning Goals ① Understand that -(으)ㄹ수록 expresses a proportional relationship between two situations — "the more X, the more Y" — and attach it correctly to verb, adjective, and 이다 stems
② Use the common -(으)면 + [same verb] + -(으)ㄹ수록 doubling pattern for emphasis, and recognize when -(으)ㄹ수록 can stand alone with two different verbs instead

1Concept Introduction — "The More... The More"

-(으)ㄹ수록 is the grammar pattern Korean uses to describe two things changing together in proportion — exactly like the English pattern "the more..., the more..." (or "the less..., the less..."). You'll hear it constantly in everyday conversation: talking about prices and quality, weather and clothing, practice and skill, or how a feeling grows the longer something continues. It attaches directly to a verb, adjective, or 이다 stem, and the clause that follows describes the resulting change.

There are two things to master here. First, the form — -(으)ㄹ수록 attaches to stems the same way -(으)ㄹ 거예요 does, so if you're comfortable with that pattern, this one will feel familiar. Second, a very common doubling pattern: when the two changing things are really "more of the same action," Korean often repeats the verb — once with -(으)면 ("if/when") and once with -(으)ㄹ수록 — to emphasize the proportional relationship, as in 보면 볼수록 ("the more you look at it..."). This chapter covers both the form rules and this doubling pattern.

Preview
날씨가 추울수록 옷을 많이 입어요. ("The colder the weather gets, the more clothes I wear.")
이 노래는 들으면 들을수록 좋아요. ("The more I listen to this song, the more I like it.")
추울수록 (cold + -(으)ㄹ수록) links weather directly to clothing choice. 들으면 들을수록 doubles the verb 듣다 ("to listen") with -(으)면 first and -(으)ㄹ수록 second, emphasizing the growing effect of repeated listening.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: attaches to the present stem, like -(으)ㄹ 거예요

-(으)ㄹ수록 attaches straight to a verb, adjective, or 이다 stem, following the same vowel/consonant rule as -(으)ㄹ 거예요 (GRAM-BEG-016). A stem ending in a vowel — or already ending in ㄹ — takes -ㄹ수록. A stem ending in any other consonant takes -을수록. 이다 always takes 일수록, regardless of whether the noun before it ends in a vowel or a consonant.

-(으)ㄹ수록 by stem type
Stem typeFormExample
Vowel-final stem-ㄹ수록크다 → 클수록
Consonant-final stem-을수록작다 → 작을수록
ㄹ-final stem-ㄹ수록 (no change)살다 → 살수록
이다/아니다일수록/아닐수록학생이다 → 학생일수록
Study Tip -(으)ㄹ수록 has no separate past-tense form — it always attaches to the present-tense stem, even when the overall sentence describes an ongoing or long-term situation, because it expresses a general proportional relationship, not a single past event. Irregular stems change the same way they do before any other -(으)ㄹ ending: 듣다 (ㄷ irregular) → 들을수록, 어렵다 (ㅂ irregular) → 어려울수록, 낫다 (ㅅ irregular) → 나을수록.
