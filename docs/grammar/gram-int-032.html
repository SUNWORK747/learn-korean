-는 바람에 Unexpected, Negative Cause

Because of Something That Went Wrong

GRAM-INT-032 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 3rd item of "6. 기타 중급 표현" (Miscellaneous Intermediate Expressions), right after GRAM-INT-031 -기 때문에 (이유, 격식)

Learning Goals ① Understand that -는 바람에 attaches to action verbs (and 있다, rarely 이다) always in the -는 form, regardless of when the cause happened, with tense marked only on the second (result) clause
② Correctly use -는 바람에 only for negative or unwanted results — never positive outcomes or future/command/suggestion endings in the result clause — and recognize that, unlike -느라고, the cause and result clauses can have different subjects
③ Distinguish -는 바람에 from -느라고 (GRAM-INT-007) and -기 때문에 (GRAM-INT-031): -느라고 needs the same subject and a time-consuming cause, -기 때문에 is neutral and formal with no restriction on the result, and -는 바람에 is for sudden or external causes with a negative result

1Concept Introduction — Because of Something That Went Wrong

Some things just happen to you: a bus is late, a phone battery dies, someone bumps into you on the street — and suddenly your whole plan falls apart. Korean has a grammar point built exactly for that kind of story: -는 바람에. The word 바람 literally means "wind" or "gust," and this ending keeps a trace of that image — something blows in unexpectedly and knocks your plans off course. It attaches to a verb describing the unexpected event, and the clause that follows almost always describes something negative or unwanted that resulted from it.

This is the third connective in the intermediate "cause and negative result" family, after -느라고 (GRAM-INT-007) and -기 때문에 (GRAM-INT-031). All three can be translated as "because," but each one carries its own restrictions on what kind of cause it allows, whether the result must be negative, and whether the same person has to be the subject of both clauses. This chapter finishes that comparison by putting all three side by side.

Preview
버스를 놓치는 바람에 학교에 지각했어요. ("Because I missed the bus, I was late for school.")
갑자기 비가 오는 바람에 옷이 다 젖었어요. ("Because it suddenly started raining, my clothes got completely soaked.")
In both sentences, something sudden and unwelcome (missing the bus, a surprise rain shower) leads directly to a negative result — being late, getting soaked. That "unlucky trigger → bad outcome" shape is the whole idea behind -는 바람에.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: action verbs (and 있다) only, always -는, tense on the result clause

-는 바람에 attaches to an action-verb stem (동사) in the same "present-looking" -는 shape every time, no matter when the event actually happened — it never takes -았/었- (past) before it. 있다 also takes -는 바람에 (있는 바람에). Attaching it to 이다 (인 바람에) is possible but rare and sounds a bit unnatural, so most learners simply avoid it. Adjectives (형용사) cannot take -는 바람에 at all — for a "because [adjective]" negative-result sentence, -아/어서 is used instead.

-는 바람에 by word type
Word typeCan attach -는 바람에?Example
Action verb (동사)Yes — stem + -는 바람에놓치다 → 놓치는 바람에
있다Yes사고가 있다 → 사고가 있는 바람에
이다Rare, avoid as a beginner-to-intermediate learner주말이다 → 주말인 바람에 (unnatural)
Adjective (형용사)No춥다 → 추운 바람에 (use 추워서 instead)
Study Tip Tense is never marked on 바람에 itself — it always shows up on the result clause instead. Compare: 늦게 일어난 바람에 학교에 늦었어요 is wrong; the correct form is 늦게 일어나는 바람에 학교에 늦었어요 ("Because I got up late, I was late for school") — 일어나다 stays in the plain -는 form, and the past tense appears only in 늦었어요.
