1Concept Introduction — The Final Chapter of the Beginner Stage

Congratulations — this is the last chapter of the entire Beginner (Pre-TOPIK) stage. You have already learned how Korean sentences are built: word order (GRAM-PRE-013), particles in general (GRAM-PRE-014), sentence-ending types (GRAM-PRE-015), questions (GRAM-PRE-016), the copula and existence verbs (GRAM-PRE-017, GRAM-PRE-018), pronouns (GRAM-PRE-019), greetings (GRAM-PRE-020), and the two number systems (GRAM-PRE-021). The one missing piece is how to say "not" and "cannot" — and that is exactly what this chapter covers.

In English, negation almost always needs a helper verb: "I go" becomes "I do not go." Korean does it differently — you simply drop a short negation word directly in front of the verb or adjective. There are two such words, and English speakers often confuse them because English "don't" and "can't" don't map onto Korean the way you might expect: 안 (a neutral, voluntary "not") and 못 (an involuntary "cannot," implying something outside your control is stopping you). Once you finish this chapter, the very next item in the whole curriculum — GRAM-BEG-001 — begins the 초급 (TOPIK I) stage with the topic particle 은/는, a particle you already got a first preview of back in GRAM-PRE-014.

Preview
안 가요 (an gayo, "[I] don't go") simply states a choice or a plain fact — you are not going, no particular reason needed.
못 가요 (mot gayo, "[I] can't go") instead tells the listener that something is preventing you from going — you're busy, sick, or blocked somehow. Same English word "can't/don't," two very different Korean words.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 안 + Verb/Adjective — Simple Negation ("not")

To make a simple negative sentence, place 안 directly before the conjugated verb or adjective. Nothing else changes — the verb/adjective keeps its normal ending. This is a much simpler process than English, which needs a helper verb like "do not" or "is not."

안 + verb/adjective placement rule
FormExampleMeaning
안 + Verb안 가요"[I] don't go" / "am not going"
안 + Verb안 먹어요"[I] don't eat [it]"
안 + Adjective안 커요"[it] isn't big"
안 + Adjective안 비싸요"[it] isn't expensive"
Key Rule Unlike English, where "not" always requires a helper verb ("do not go"), Korean 안 attaches straight onto the front of the verb/adjective with no other change needed. Word order stays: Subject → Object → 안 + Verb/Adjective.
