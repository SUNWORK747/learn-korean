-더라도 Concession, "Even If"

The Everyday Advanced "Even If" — Common, Natural, and Everywhere in TOPIK II Speech and Writing

1Concept Introduction — The "Even If" You'll Actually Use Most

In the last chapter you learned -(으)ㄹ지라도, the strongest and most literary way to say "even if" in Korean — the kind of concession you'd hear in a speech, a proverb, or a court ruling. -더라도 is its everyday cousin. It carries the same core meaning — "even if / even though [X] may be true, [Y] still holds" — but without the dramatic, rhetorical weight. -더라도 is by far the most common advanced-level concessive: fluent adult speakers use it constantly in both fairly formal conversation and general writing, wherever the basic -아/어도 feels a little too plain for the situation.

The form is refreshingly simple compared to some of the concessives you'll meet in this section: -더라도 attaches directly to a stem — verb, adjective, or 이다 — the same way, no matter the word type, and (in its basic present form) without a separate past-tense shape. It's very often paired with 아무리 ("no matter how ~") at the front of the clause to intensify the concession, a pattern you'll see throughout this chapter.

Preview
비가 오더라도 소풍은 갈 거예요. ("Even if it rains, we'll still go on the picnic.")
아무리 바쁘더라도 밥은 먹어야죠. ("No matter how busy you are, you should still eat.")
Both sentences accept the situation in the -더라도 clause as possible (or even likely) and state that the main clause happens anyway. Notice 아무리 in the second sentence — it strengthens "even if busy" into "no matter how busy."

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: one shape for every word type, plus a past option

Unlike -는데/-(으)ㄴ데 (GRAM-INT-001), -더라도 does not change shape depending on whether it attaches to a verb, an adjective, or 이다 — it's simply stem + -더라도 across the board. To express a counterfactual concession about something already finished ("even if it had happened"), attach it to the past stem instead: -았/었더라도.

-더라도 by word type, present and past
Word typePresentPast (counterfactual)Example
Verb (동사)-더라도-았/었더라도가더라도 / 갔더라도
있다/없다-더라도-았/었더라도있더라도 / 없었더라도
Adjective (형용사)-더라도-았/었더라도바쁘더라도 / 바빴더라도
이다/아니다(이)더라도/아니더라도이었/였더라도학생이더라도 / 학생이었더라도
Study Tip -더라도 historically contains the retrospective marker -더-, but in modern Korean it functions as one fused concessive ending — you don't need to have personally witnessed or experienced anything for it to apply. After a noun ending in a vowel, the 이 of 이다 is often dropped in casual speech: 친구이더라도 is commonly shortened to 친구더라도 ("even if [it's] a friend"). After a consonant-final noun, the 이 stays: 학생이더라도, never 학생더라도.
