-지만 Connective: Contrast ("But")

Joining Two Clauses That Disagree with Each Other

GRAM-BEG-024 Elementary (TOPIK I) 20 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — second item of this stage's Connective Endings section
CategoryConnective Endings > 3. Connective Endings (연결어미)
Learning Goals ① Attach -지만 to any verb, adjective, or 이다 stem (no batchim distinction) to connect two clauses that contrast, meaning "but/although"
② Recognize that, unlike -고, -지만 freely accepts tense markers (-았/었-, -겠-) directly before it, because the two contrasted clauses often belong to different times or situations
③ Distinguish -지만 (contrast, "but") from -고 (neutral listing, "and") and choose the right one for a given relationship between two ideas

1Concept Introduction — Saying "But"

In GRAM-BEG-023 you learned -고, the neutral connective that simply lists two things together. But not every pair of ideas agrees with each other — sometimes the second clause pushes back against the first: "This restaurant is expensive, but it's delicious." That's the job of -지만, the second connective ending in this section, meaning "but/although."

Structurally, -지만 is just as easy to attach as -고 — no batchim rule to check. The one thing that's genuinely different is how it treats tense, which you'll see in §2.2: unlike -고's habit of marking tense only once at the end, -지만 is comfortable taking -았/었- or -겠- directly before it.

Preview
이 식당은 비싸지만 맛있어요. ("This restaurant is expensive, but it's delicious.")
어제는 비가 왔지만 오늘은 맑아요. ("It rained yesterday, but today is clear.")
In the second sentence, past tense (왔) attaches right before -지만, because the first clause (yesterday) and the second clause (today) describe different times.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Attaching -지만 — no batchim rule needed

-지만 attaches directly to a verb, adjective, or 이다 ("to be") stem: stem + -지만. Exactly like -고, it never changes depending on batchim, and there's no vowel-harmony rule to check.

Stem + -지만 — always the same shape
Word (dictionary form)Stem+ -지만Meaning
가다 (to go)가가지만"go, but..."
먹다 (to eat)먹먹지만"eat, but..."
좋다 (to be good)좋좋지만"be good, but..."
학생이다 (to be a student)학생이학생이지만"be a student, but..."
Study Tip Notice -지만 can attach straight onto 이다 too, not just action/description verbs — useful for sentences like "I'm a student, but I work part-time" (저는 학생이지만 아르바이트를 해요).
