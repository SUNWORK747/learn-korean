-(으)ㄴ/는데도 Even Though... Still (Concessive)

When the Expected Outcome Doesn't Happen

GRAM-INT-011 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 25 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 11th and final item of the "연결어미 확장" (Connective Endings Expansion) section, right after GRAM-INT-010 -는 김에
CategoryConnective Endings Expansion > 1. 연결어미 확장
Learning Goals ① Understand that -는데도/-(으)ㄴ데도/인데도 is built from -는데 (GRAM-INT-001) plus 도 ("even," "also"), creating a concessive meaning: "even though X, Y still happens" — an unexpected or contrary-to-expectation result
② Correctly attach -는데도 to verbs (and 있다/없다), -(으)ㄴ데도 to adjectives, and 인데도 to 이다, in both present and past tense — the exact same conjugation pattern as -는데, with 도 simply added on
③ Distinguish -는데도 from -지만 and from plain -는데, recognizing that -는데도 always signals a result that goes against what the first clause would lead you to expect

1Concept Introduction — Building a Stronger "But" from an Ending You Already Know

This chapter is good news: you don't need to learn a brand-new ending from scratch. Back in GRAM-INT-001 you learned -는데/-(으)ㄴ데, the connective that either sets the background for a comment or hints at a mild contrast. -(으)ㄴ/는데도 is simply that same connective with 도 ("even," "also") attached to the end. Adding 도 sharpens the meaning into something much more specific and emphatic: "even though X (happened/is true), Y still happens anyway" — a result that goes directly against what you'd expect given the first clause. This kind of meaning is called 양보 (concessive) in Korean grammar terminology: the first clause "concedes" a fact, and the second clause shows that the fact didn't change the outcome.

Because the form is just -는데/-(으)ㄴ데/인데 + 도, almost everything about how to attach it carries over directly from what you already know. The real work in this chapter is learning the new, sharper meaning, and learning exactly where -는데도 fits compared to the plain -지만 you learned in Elementary and the -는데 you just reviewed. As the last item in the "연결어미 확장" (Connective Endings Expansion) section, this chapter also closes out that whole family of eleven connectives before moving on to expressions of conjecture and intention.

Preview
비가 오는데도 우산을 안 가져갔어요. ("Even though it was raining, I didn't bring an umbrella.")
열심히 공부했는데도 시험에 떨어졌어요. ("Even though I studied hard, I failed the exam.")
오는데도 (오다 + -는데 + 도) and 공부했는데도 (공부하다 + -았는데 + 도) both describe a result — not bringing an umbrella despite the rain, failing despite hard study — that a listener would not expect given the first clause. That's the extra punch 도 adds on top of plain -는데.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: -는데 (GRAM-INT-001) + 도, nothing new to memorize

Every rule from -는데/-(으)ㄴ데/인데 applies here unchanged — just add 도 at the end. Verbs (and 있다/없다) take -는데도 in the present tense; adjectives take -(으)ㄴ데도; and 이다/아니다 take 인데도/아닌데도. In the past tense, everything again collapses to the same -았/었는데도 pattern, regardless of word type.

-는데도/-(으)ㄴ데도/인데도 by word type and tense
Word typePresentPastExample
Verb (동사)-는데도-았/었는데도가는데도 / 갔는데도
있다/없다-는데도-았/었는데도있는데도 / 없었는데도
Adjective (형용사)-(으)ㄴ데도-았/었는데도추운데도 / 추웠는데도
이다/아니다인데도/아닌데도이었는데도/였는데도학생인데도 / 학생이었는데도
Study Tip Because -는데도 is literally -는데 + 도, every conjugation quirk you already learned in GRAM-INT-001 carries over untouched: 있다/없다 (and "있다-compound" adjectives like 맛있다, 재미있다) still take -는데도, not -(으)ㄴ데도 — so 맛있는데도 and 재미있는데도, never 맛있은데도. Irregular verbs and adjectives (like 춥다 → 추운데도) also conjugate exactly the way they already do before -는데; 도 never changes that step.
