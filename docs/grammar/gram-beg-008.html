만 Only

The Opposite of 도 — Exclusive, Not Additive

GRAM-BEG-008 Elementary (TOPIK I) 15 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — direct counterpart to GRAM-BEG-007 도
CategoryParticles > 1. Particles
Learning Goals ① State that 만 means "only" and attaches directly to a noun
② Understand that 만, like 도 (GRAM-BEG-007), REPLACES 은/는/이/가/을/를 on that noun rather than stacking with them
③ Contrast 만 (exclusive, "only") with 도 (additive, "also") — same replacement mechanism, opposite meaning

1Concept Introduction — Only One Thing, Nothing Else

In GRAM-BEG-007 you learned 도, the particle that means "also / too" and adds a noun to a group. 만 does the opposite job: it means "only" and narrows a sentence down to a single noun, excluding everything else. Like every particle you've studied so far, it attaches directly to the end of a noun with no space in writing.

The most important thing to notice about 만 is that it behaves exactly like 도 in one key way: it doesn't sit alongside 은/는/이/가/을/를 on the same noun — it takes their place. You already learned this replacement mechanism for 도; now you'll see the very same mechanism used to express the opposite meaning.

Preview
저만 학생이에요. ("Only I am a student.")
Compare this to 저도 학생이에요. ("I am also a student.") Same noun, same replaced particle slot, opposite meaning — that contrast is the heart of this chapter.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 만 = "only" — one form, no batchim rule

Unlike 은/는 (GRAM-BEG-001), which changes shape depending on whether the noun ends in a consonant or a vowel, 만 has just one fixed form. It attaches the same way after every noun, regardless of batchim — one less rule to memorize than particles like 은/는.

만 attaching to different nouns
Noun+ 만Meaning
저 (I)저만"only I / only me"
커피 (coffee)커피만"only coffee"
오늘 (today)오늘만"only today"
학생 (student)학생만"only the student(s)"
