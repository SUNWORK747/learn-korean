1Concept Introduction — Guessing From What You Can See or Hear

You just learned -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 (GRAM-INT-012), the all-purpose way to say "I think..." or "it seems like..." in Korean. -나 보다/-(으)ㄴ가 보다 is a close cousin with a sharper edge: it's what Korean speakers reach for specifically when they've noticed some concrete clue — a sound, a sight, a situation — and are drawing a conclusion from it on the spot. Think of the English phrases "it looks like..." or "I guess... (judging by that)" rather than a plain, evidence-free "I think." If you see everyone outside holding umbrellas, you don't just think it's raining — you conclude it from the umbrellas. That's exactly the feeling -나 보다/-(으)ㄴ가 보다 captures.

Like -는데 (GRAM-INT-001), this pattern changes shape depending on the word type it attaches to: verbs and 있다/없다 take one form, adjectives take another, and 이다 takes a third — but the past tense collapses all of them into a single shared pattern. This chapter covers those form rules first, then focuses on the meaning itself and how it's different from -(으)ㄹ 것 같다.

Preview
창문에 물방울이 맺힌 거 보니 밖에 비가 오나 봐요. ("Seeing water droplets on the window, it looks like it's raining outside.")
사람들이 다 두꺼운 코트를 입은 거 보니 오늘 많이 추운가 봐요. ("Seeing that everyone's wearing thick coats, it seems it's really cold today.")
오나 봐요 (verb 오다 + -나 보다) and 추운가 봐요 (adjective 춥다 + -(으)ㄴ가 보다) are both conclusions drawn from something specific the speaker just noticed — condensation on the glass, people bundled up in coats — not just a hunch pulled out of nowhere.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: it depends on word type and tense

Just like -는데, this pattern has one shared meaning but several different faces. Verbs (and the special pair 있다/없다) take -나 보다 in the present tense; adjectives take -(으)ㄴ가 보다; and 이다/아니다 take 인가 보다/아닌가 보다. In the past tense, every word type simplifies to the same -았/었나 보다 pattern — the verb/adjective/이다 distinction disappears once you're in the past.

-나 보다/-(으)ㄴ가 보다 by word type and tense
Word typePresentPastExample
Verb (동사)-나 보다-았/었나 보다가나 보다 / 갔나 보다
있다/없다-나 보다-았/었나 보다있나 보다 / 없었나 보다
Adjective (형용사)-(으)ㄴ가 보다-았/었나 보다좋은가 보다 / 좋았나 보다
이다/아니다인가 보다/아닌가 보다이었/였나 보다학생인가 보다 / 학생이었나 보다
Study Tip 있다/없다 behave like verbs and take -나 보다 (있나 보다, 없나 보다) — not -(으)ㄴ가 보다 — the same rule you already saw with -는데. This also carries over to "있다-compound" adjectives like 맛있다 ("to be tasty") and 재미있다 ("to be fun"): they conjugate like 있다, so it's 맛있나 보다 and 재미있나 보다, never 맛있은가 보다. Also watch for ㄹ-irregular adjectives: the ㄹ drops before -(으)ㄴ가 보다, so 길다 ("to be long") becomes 긴가 보다, and 힘들다 ("to be hard/tough") becomes 힘든가 보다.
