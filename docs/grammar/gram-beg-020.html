-네요 Exclamation/Realization

-네요 (감탄)

Reacting to Something You Just Noticed

GRAM-BEG-020 Elementary (TOPIK I) 15 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급)
CategorySentence-Final Endings > 2. Sentence-Final Endings
Learning Goals ① Attach -네요 to a verb/adjective stem (or 이다) to express a fresh realization or mild surprise upon directly observing something
② Distinguish -네요 from plain -아/어요 (GRAM-BEG-013): -네요 specifically signals the speaker just noticed/realized this fact in the moment, not simply stating already-known information
③ Use -네요 correctly in natural exclamatory sentences with common adjectives

1Concept Introduction — Reacting in the Moment

Imagine you step outside and the weather turns out to be surprisingly nice, or you take a bite of a friend's cooking and it's much better than you expected. In English, you might just say "Oh, this is good!" with a surprised tone of voice. Korean has a dedicated grammar ending for exactly this feeling: -네요.

-네요 attaches to the end of a verb or adjective stem to mark that the speaker is noticing or realizing something right now, as a direct, fresh reaction — not simply reciting a fact they already knew. You already learned the plain, neutral statement ending -아/어요 in GRAM-BEG-013; -네요 adds a layer of "wow, I just noticed this" on top of that same basic sentence.

Preview
오늘 날씨가 좋네요! ("The weather's nice today! [I just noticed]") — said the moment you step outside and feel the nice weather, not as a plain report of something you already knew this morning.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Attaching -네요 — no batchim rule to worry about

Unlike many endings you've learned so far, -네요 attaches directly to a verb or adjective stem with no batchim distinction and no vowel-harmony choice to make — it is always just stem + 네요, whether the stem ends in a consonant or a vowel.

Stem + 네요 — the same ending every time
Dictionary formStem+ 네요Meaning
좋다 (adj., "to be good")좋좋네요"Oh, it's good!"
크다 (adj., "to be big")크크네요"Wow, it's really big!"
오다 (verb, "to come")오오네요"Oh, here it comes!"
이다 (copula, "to be")이학생이네요"Oh, [you're] a student!"

For a past realization, simply insert the past tense marker -았/었- (GRAM-BEG-015) before -네요: 오다 → 왔 + 네요 = 벌써 왔네요! ("Oh, you're already here!").

Study Tip Because -네요 attaches the same way regardless of the stem's final sound, it's one of the easiest endings to form — the hard part isn't the grammar, it's remembering when to reach for it instead of plain -아/어요. That's exactly what the next section covers.
