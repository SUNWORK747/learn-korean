-다고 하다 Reporting What Someone Said

Indirect Speech for Statements — Opening the Quotation Series

1Concept Introduction — Telling Someone What Someone Else Said

In English, when you report what someone said, you usually don't repeat their exact words: instead of "She said, 'I'm tired.'" you often say "She said she was tired" — the quotation marks disappear, and the sentence gets folded into your own. Korean does something very similar, but it uses a dedicated grammatical ending to do the folding, and — unlike English — it uses a different ending for each sentence type. This chapter opens a four-part mini-series on exactly that system: -다고 하다 for statements (this chapter), followed by -냐고 하다 for questions (GRAM-INT-021), -자고 하다 for suggestions (GRAM-INT-022), and -(으)라고 하다 for commands (GRAM-INT-023). Once you've seen all four, the family resemblance — every one of them ends in 고 하다 — will make a lot more sense.

The core idea of -다고 하다 is simple: take the sentence being reported, strip it down to its plain, dictionary-style ending (the same "diary style" you may have seen written as 한다체), and attach 고 하다 ("to say that…"). Because the original speaker's politeness level gets stripped away in this process, "내일 갈게요," "내일 갈래," and "내일 갑니다" (three very different speech levels, all meaning roughly "I'll go tomorrow") would all be reported the exact same way: 내일 간다고 해요. The reporting verb 하다 itself can then be conjugated to whatever politeness level you want to speak in — 해요, 했어요, 합니다, 한다, and so on.

Preview
민수 씨가 "내일 학교에 가요." (Minsu: "I'm going to school tomorrow.")
→ 민수 씨가 내일 학교에 간다고 해요. ("Minsu says he's going to school tomorrow.")
날씨가 춥다고 했어요. ("She said [that] the weather was cold.")
간다고 (동사 가다 + -ㄴ다고) reports a statement made with a verb; 춥다고 (형용사 춥다 + -다고) reports one made with an adjective. Same job, slightly different shape — that's what Section 2 sorts out.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: it depends on word type and tense

-다고 하다 is built from the plain/dictionary-style declarative ending (한다체) plus 고 하다. That plain ending itself changes shape depending on the word type: verbs take -ㄴ다/는다, adjectives keep the dictionary form -다 unchanged, and 이다/아니다 take -(이)라/아니라. Attach 고 하다 to any of these and you get the reported-speech form. In the past tense, everything simplifies to the familiar -았/었다고 하다, and for the future, -(으)ㄹ 거라고 하다 is used across word types.

있다/없다-다고 하다-았/었다고 하다-(으)ㄹ 거라고 하다있다고 하다 / 없다고 하다
Adjective (형용사)-다고 하다-았/었다고 하다-(으)ㄹ 거라고 하다예쁘다고 하다 / 춥다고 하다
이다/아니다-(이)라고 하다 / 아니라고 하다이었/였다고 하다일 거라고 하다학생이라고 하다 / 의사라고 하다
Study Tip Watch the reversal from GRAM-INT-001: there, 있다/없다 behaved like verbs and took -는데. Here, for reported speech, 있다/없다 behave like adjectives and take plain -다고 — it's 있다고 하다, never 있는다고 하다. That's because 있다's own plain declarative form is simply "있다," unchanged, the same way an adjective's is. This also covers 있다-compounds like 재미있다 and 맛있다: 재미있다고 하다, 맛있다고 하다 — not 재미있는다고.
