Conditional Concession — Permission or Fact Granted, Qualification Attached

1Concept Introduction — Granting Something, Then Immediately Qualifying It

Welcome to the final stretch of the curriculum: 담화·논증 표현 (Discourse/Argumentation Expressions), the fifth and last category of Advanced Korean grammar. The four items in this category (GRAM-ADV-020 through GRAM-ADV-023) are the connectives and expressions you'll see constantly in essays, news articles, official documents, and formal debate — the "written argument" register that TOPIK II 5–6급 readers and writers are expected to command. -되 is this category's opening item, and it's a classic one: you'll recognize it instantly once you start reading Korean editorials, regulations, or public notices, because it appears in almost every sentence of the form "X is allowed/true, but here's the condition."

-되 grants something — a permission, a fact, an allowance — in its first clause, and then immediately attaches a qualification, restriction, or exception in the second clause. Think of maxims like "허락하되 조건이 있다" ("It is permitted, but there is a condition") or museum signs that read "사진을 촬영하되 플래시는 사용하지 마세요" ("You may take photos, but do not use flash"). The structure always has this same two-part shape: grant, then qualify. Unlike the everyday -지만 you already know, -되 is strictly a written, formal register connective — you won't hear it in casual conversation, but you'll see it everywhere in argumentative and official writing.

Preview
자유는 보장되되 책임이 따른다. ("Freedom is guaranteed, but responsibility follows.")
사용하되 원본을 훼손하지 마세요. ("You may use it, but do not damage the original.")
보장되되 (보장되다, "to be guaranteed" + -되) grants that freedom is guaranteed, then the second clause immediately attaches the condition that responsibility comes with it. 사용하되 (사용하다, "to use" + -되) grants permission to use something, then the second clause — a negative imperative — states the restriction: don't damage the original. Same two-part logic both times: grant, then qualify.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: stem + -되, with no batchim variation

-되 is refreshingly simple in form compared to most connectives you've studied: it attaches directly to the verb or adjective stem, with no change based on whether the stem ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel — unlike -(으)면 or -(으)니, there is no 으 to insert. 이다 becomes 이되, and 아니다 becomes 아니되. -되 is also not combined with the past tense marker -았/었- in typical usage: it expresses a general, timeless statement — the kind of maxim, rule, or regulation that holds true regardless of when it's read — so it almost always attaches straight to the plain present stem.

-되 by word type
Word typeFormExample
Verb (동사)stem + -되사용하다 → 사용하되 / 지키다 → 지키되
있다/없다stem + -되있다 → 있되 / 없다 → 없되
Adjective (형용사)stem + -되쉽다 → 쉽되 / 좋다 → 좋되
이다/아니다이되/아니되무료이다 → 무료이되 / 사실이 아니다 → 사실이 아니되
Study Tip Because -되 attaches directly with no batchim rule to memorize, the form itself is easy — the real work is recognizing the register. -되 belongs to formal written Korean (문어체): essays, news, laws, regulations, museum/park signage, and argumentative writing. Using it in casual speech would sound noticeably stiff, the same way quoting a legal document mid-conversation would in English.
