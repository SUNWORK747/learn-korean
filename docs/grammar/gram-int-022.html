-자고 하다 Reported Suggestions

Quoting a "Let's..." Proposal Indirectly

1Concept Introduction — Quoting a Proposal Indirectly

You've now learned how to report a plain statement with -다고 하다 (GRAM-INT-020) and a question with -냐고 하다 (GRAM-INT-021). Korean has a third dedicated quoting pattern for a very common type of sentence: a suggestion or proposal — the kind of sentence that in direct speech ends in -자 ("let's…"), the more formal -읍시다/-ㅂ시다, or even a suggestion phrased as a question with -을까요? ("shall we…?"). All three of those direct forms collapse into the same reported pattern: verb stem + -자고 하다.

This is the third part of a four-part quotation-particle series: statements (-다고 하다) → questions (-냐고 하다) → suggestions (-자고 하다, this chapter) → commands (-(으)라고 하다, up next in GRAM-INT-023). The good news is that -자고 하다 is one of the simplest of the four to form: there's no tense to worry about and no split between verbs/adjectives/이다, because you can only "suggest" that someone do something — suggestions are always about action verbs.

Preview
민수: "우리 같이 점심 먹자." (Minsu: "Let's eat lunch together.")
→ 민수가 저한테 같이 점심 먹자고 했어요. ("Minsu suggested that we eat lunch together.")
선생님: "다 같이 청소합시다." (Teacher: "Let's all clean up together.")
→ 선생님이 다 같이 청소하자고 하셨어요. ("The teacher suggested that we all clean up together.")
Both the plain -자 suggestion and the more formal -읍시다 suggestion turn into the exact same reported form: stem + -자고 하다.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: verb stem + -자고 하다

Unlike -다고 하다 and -냐고 하다, which change shape depending on word type and tense, -자고 하다 has only one shape: attach -자고 하다 directly to the verb stem, with no linking vowel needed and no tense marker, because a suggestion always points toward a future action, never the past. Any of the three direct-speech suggestion endings below collapses into this same reported form.

Direct suggestion endings → reported form
Direct speech endingExample (direct)Reported form
-자 (plain suggestion)"같이 가자."같이 가자고 하다
-읍시다/-ㅂ시다 (formal suggestion)"같이 갑시다."같이 가자고 하다
-을까요?/-ㄹ까요? (suggesting as a question)"같이 갈까요?"같이 가자고 하다
-자고 하다 with common verbs
VerbDirectReported
가다 (to go)"가자."가자고 하다
먹다 (to eat)"먹자."먹자고 하다
만들다 (to make)"만들자."만들자고 하다
쉬다 (to rest)"쉬자."쉬자고 하다
Study Tip Because a suggestion is inherently about an action someone hasn't done yet, -자고 하다 is only ever used with action verbs (동사). It never attaches to adjectives or 이다 — you can't say "let's be pretty" or "let's be a student" as a genuine proposal, so those simply don't occur with this pattern.
