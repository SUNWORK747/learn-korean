보다 (비교) Comparison

“Than” — and the End of the Particles Section

GRAM-BEG-012 Elementary (TOPIK I) 15 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — final grammar item of the Particles section
CategoryParticles > 1. Particles
Learning Goals ① Attach 보다 directly after the noun being compared against, meaning “than”
② Optionally pair 보다 with 더 (“more”) before the adjective for emphasis
③ Recognize this is the final chapter of the Particles (조사) section — the next chapter begins Sentence-Final Endings (종결어미)

1Concept Introduction — Comparing Two Things

So far every particle in this section has attached to a single noun to mark its role in the sentence — topic (은/는), subject (이/가), object (을/를), and so on. 보다 does something a little different: it attaches to a noun to set up a comparison, meaning “than.” Whatever noun 보다 attaches to is the thing being compared against.

보다 is also special for a practical reason: it closes out the Particles (조사) section of this course (GRAM-BEG-001 through GRAM-BEG-012). Once you finish this chapter, the next chapter, GRAM-BEG-013, shifts focus entirely — from particles that attach to nouns, to Sentence-Final Endings (종결어미), starting with -아/어요, the polite casual style you have already been reading in every single example sentence throughout this course.

Preview
저는 커피보다 차를 좋아해요. (“I like tea more than coffee.”)
오늘은 어제보다 추워요. (“Today is colder than yesterday.”) — notice 보다 attaches directly after the noun being compared against (커피, 어제), with no space and no batchim rule to worry about.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Attaching 보다 — no batchim distinction

Unlike 은/는 or 이/가, 보다 has only one form. It attaches directly after the noun being compared against, regardless of whether that noun ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel — there is no batchim-based alternate spelling to learn here, which makes 보다 one of the easier particles to use correctly.

보다 — always the same form
Noun endingParticleExampleMeaning
Consonant (받침 있음)보다어제보다“than yesterday”
Vowel (받침 없음)보다커피보다“than coffee”
Consonant (받침 있음)보다동생보다“than [my] younger sibling”
Vowel (받침 없음)보다버스보다“than the bus”
Study Tip Every particle you have learned so far in this section (은/는, 이/가, 을/를, 으로, etc.) changes shape depending on the batchim of the noun it attaches to. 보다 is the one exception among comparison markers — it is always just 보다, no matter what. One less rule to memorize.
