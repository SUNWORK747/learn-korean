-겠- Conjecture & Volition

Guessing From What You See, and Declaring What You'll Do

GRAM-INT-014 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 3rd item of the "추측·의지 표현" (Conjecture & Volition Expressions) section, coming right after GRAM-INT-013 -나 보다/-(으)ㄴ가 보다

Learning Goals ① Understand the two core functions of -겠-: making a conjecture based on what you can directly see/hear/feel right now, and stating the speaker's own volition, intention, or a formal promise
② Correctly attach -겠- to any verb, adjective, or 이다 stem in the present tense (-겠어요/-겠습니다) and form the past-guess pattern -았/었겠- for guessing about something already finished
③ Distinguish -겠- from -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 (GRAM-INT-012) and -나 보다/-(으)ㄴ가 보다 (GRAM-INT-013), and recognize common fixed expressions like 잘 먹겠습니다, 알겠습니다, and 모르겠어요

1Concept Introduction — Guessing, and Declaring Your Will

-겠- is one of the most iconic pre-final endings in Korean — it slots directly into a verb, adjective, or 이다 stem, right before the sentence-ending, and it does two jobs that seem unrelated at first but both come from the same root idea of "what's about to happen or what's true, as I see it right now." You've already met two other ways to guess in Korean: -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 (GRAM-INT-012), the general, all-purpose guess, and -나 보다/-(으)ㄴ가 보다 (GRAM-INT-013), a guess built on some outside clue you noticed. -겠- adds a third flavor of conjecture — one that feels like an instant, in-the-moment reaction to something right in front of you — plus a completely separate meaning that neither of those other two forms has at all: stating the speaker's own intention or will.

Because of that second meaning, -겠- shows up constantly in formal, polished Korean: weather forecasts (내일은 전국에 비가 오겠습니다), news broadcasts, formal self-introductions, and workplace promises (최선을 다하겠습니다, "I will do my best"). It also hides inside a handful of fixed expressions every learner picks up early — 잘 먹겠습니다, 알겠습니다, 모르겠어요 — often long before they consciously know the grammar behind them. This chapter walks through the form, both meanings, and how to tell -겠- apart from the two conjecture forms you already know.

Preview
비가 오네요. 오늘 소풍은 힘들겠어요. ("Oh, it's raining. Today's picnic is probably going to be tough.")
제가 내일까지 끝내겠습니다. ("I will finish it by tomorrow.")
힘들겠어요 is a conjecture — the speaker looks at the rain right now and reacts with a guess about the picnic. 끝내겠습니다 is volition — the speaker is promising to finish the work themselves. Same -겠-, two very different jobs.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: one shape, attaches to any stem

Unlike -는데/-(으)ㄴ데 (GRAM-INT-001), -겠- doesn't change shape depending on word type or final consonant — it is always just -겠-, attached directly after the stem of a verb, adjective, or 이다, and then followed by an ending such as -어요, -습니다, -지요, or the plain -다. To guess about something already finished (a past guess), add the regular past tense marker -았/었- before -겠-, giving the pattern -았/었겠-.

-겠- across word types, present and past guess
Word typePresentPast guessExample
Verb (동사)가다 → 가겠어요갔겠어요"will probably go / must be going" — "must have gone"
Adjective (형용사)힘들다 → 힘들겠어요힘들었겠어요"must be hard" — "must have been hard"
이다학생이다 → 학생이겠어요학생이었겠어요"must be a student" — "must have been a student"
있다/없다있다 → 있겠어요있었겠어요"there will probably be" — "there must have been"
Study Tip -겠- never changes for spelling or vowel harmony — it's the same three letters every time, no matter what stem it attaches to. This makes it one of the easiest endings to form correctly; the real challenge with -겠- is choosing the right meaning for the context, not the form itself.

3Examples

3.1 -겠- in real sentences: conjecture vs. volition

KoreanMeaningFunction
하늘을 보니 비가 오겠어요."Looking at the sky, it's going to rain."Conjecture (direct evidence)
제가 짐을 들겠습니다."I'll carry the bags."Volition (offering to help)
시험이 그렇게 어려웠어요? 정말 힘들었겠어요."The test was that hard? That must have been really tough."Conjecture (past guess)
앞으로 더 노력하겠습니다."I will work harder from now on."Volition (formal promise)
이 음식 정말 맛있겠다!"This food looks so delicious!"Conjecture (casual exclamation)
