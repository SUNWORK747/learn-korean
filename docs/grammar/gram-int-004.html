-자마자 Immediate Sequential Action

The Moment One Thing Happens, Right Away

GRAM-INT-004 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 4th item of this stage, in the "연결어미 확장" (Connective Endings Expansion) section, following GRAM-INT-003 -든지
CategoryConnective Endings Expansion > 1. 연결어미 확장
Learning Goals ① Understand that -자마자 means the second action happens immediately, with virtually no gap in time, right after the first action finishes
② Correctly attach -자마자 directly to the plain verb stem — never after a tense marker like -았/었-, and only with action verbs (동사), not adjectives
③ Distinguish -자마자 from -(으)ㄴ 후에, recognizing that -자마자 emphasizes near-zero gap while -(으)ㄴ 후에 simply states one action follows another, with any length of gap allowed

1Concept Introduction — The Moment One Thing Happens, Right Away

-자마자 is how Korean speakers say "as soon as" — it links two actions so tightly that the second one starts the instant the first one finishes, with almost no gap in between. If you tell a friend 집에 도착하자마자 전화했어요 ("I called as soon as I got home"), you're saying the phone call happened right at the moment of arrival, not five minutes later. This immediacy is the whole point of -자마자, and it's what sets it apart from other "after" expressions you may already know, like -기 전에/-(으)ㄴ 후에 (GRAM-BEG-037), which just describes a before/after order without promising anything about how much time passed in between.

The good news is that the form is refreshingly simple: -자마자 always attaches to the plain, tense-less verb stem, no matter what tense the overall sentence is in, and no matter whether the stem ends in a vowel or a consonant — there's no batchim-based variation to memorize, unlike -는데/-(으)ㄴ데 (GRAM-INT-001). The two clauses can also have completely different subjects, which makes -자마자 useful for describing chain reactions between people: one person's action triggers another person's reaction, right away.

Preview
수업이 끝나자마자 친구들과 카페에 갔어요. ("As soon as class ended, I went to a cafe with my friends.")
아기는 눕자마자 잠들었어요. ("The baby fell asleep as soon as it lay down.")
끝나자마자 (끝나다, "to end" + -자마자) and 눕자마자 (눕다, "to lie down" + -자마자) both attach straight to the plain stem — no past tense marker, even though the whole sentence describes something that already happened. The tense lives in the second clause (갔어요, 잠들었어요), not in -자마자 itself.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: plain verb stem + -자마자, no exceptions

Attach -자마자 directly to the plain dictionary stem of an action verb (동사). There is no vowel-harmony choice (아/어) and no batchim-based change (으 insertion) to worry about — it is always simply stem + 자마자. Crucially, -자마자 never follows a tense marker: you cannot say 도착했자마자 or 도착하겠자마자. The overall sentence tense is carried entirely by the second clause, so the same -자마자 clause can lead into a past-, present-, or future-tense sentence.

-자마자 attachment (plain stem only, no tense marker)
Verb (stem)+ -자마자Example sentence (2nd clause tense)
도착하다 (to arrive)도착하자마자도착하자마자 전화했어요. (past)
끝나다 (to end)끝나자마자끝나자마자 나갈 거예요. (future)
먹다 (to eat)먹자마자먹자마자 이를 닦아요. (present habit)
듣다 (to hear)듣자마자듣자마자 눈물이 났어요. (past)
Study Tip -자마자 is only used with action verbs (동사), not adjectives (형용사) and not 이다. Adjectives describe a state, not a completed event, so there's no clear "moment it happens" for -자마자 to attach to — you can't say 예쁘자마자 or 학생이자마자. If you need to describe a state changing instantly, Korean uses other structures instead, which are outside the scope of this chapter.
