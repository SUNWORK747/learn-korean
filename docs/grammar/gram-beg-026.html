-(으)니까 Because ("Since / As")

A Reason That Leads to a Suggestion, Command, or Request

GRAM-BEG-026 Elementary (TOPIK I) 25 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급)
CategoryConnective Endings > 3. 연결어미 (Connective Endings)
Learning Goals ① Attach -(으)니까 to a verb/adjective stem using the batchim rule (consonant-ending stem + -으니까, vowel-ending stem + -니까), including ㄹ-stems, 있다/없다, and 이다
② Recognize that -(으)니까 gives a subjective, speaker-felt reason and must be used (not -아/어서) when the following clause is a suggestion, command, request, or proposal
③ Compare -(으)니까 with -아/어서: only -(으)니까 can be followed by an imperative (-(으)세요) or propositive (-(으)ㅂ시다/-(으)ㄹ까요) ending, and only -(으)니까 can attach after past tense -았/었-

1Concept Introduction — A Reason That Calls for Action

This chapter's section, "Connective Endings (연결어미)," gives you several ways to say "because" in Korean, and -(으)니까 is one of the two most common at this level (the other, -아/어서, sits right before it in this section). Both mean roughly "because/since," but they are not interchangeable — each has a different job depending on what comes after the reason.

-(으)니까's defining trait is this: it is the connective you reach for when your reason is followed by a suggestion, command, or request — things like "let's go," "please rest," or "shall we eat?" This makes it extremely common in everyday speech, because people constantly explain why right before telling someone what to do.

Preview
비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요. ("Since it's raining, take an umbrella.")
배가 고프니까 같이 밥 먹을까요? ("Since I'm hungry, shall we eat together?")
Both sentences pair a reason with a command or suggestion — exactly the job -(으)니까 does.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Attaching -(으)니까 — the batchim rule

-(으)니까 follows the same batchim (final-consonant) rule you already know from other -(으) endings: a stem ending in a consonant inserts a connecting 으 before 니까; a stem ending in a vowel attaches 니까 directly.

Stem type + -(으)니까
Stem typeVerb+ -(으)니까Meaning
Consonant-ending먹다 (to eat)먹으니까"since (I/you) eat"
Vowel-ending가다 (to go)가니까"since (I/you) go"
ㄹ-ending (ㄹ drops)살다 (to live)사니까"since (I/you) live"
있다/없다없다 (to not exist/have)없으니까"since there isn't/(I) don't have"
이다 (copula)학생이다 (to be a student)학생이니까"since (I am) a student"

Notice the ㄹ-stem row: 살다 becomes 사니까, not 살으니까 — 니까 begins with ㄴ, and stems ending in ㄹ drop that ㄹ before endings starting with ㄴ, ㅂ, ㅅ, or 오. Past tense works the same way with -았/었으니까: 비가 왔으니까 ("since it rained").

Study Tip -(으)니까 uses the exact same 으-insertion pattern you've already seen in endings like -(으)세요: consonant stem → add 으 first; vowel stem → skip straight to 니까. You are not learning a new rule, just applying a familiar one to a new ending.

3Examples

3.1 -(으)니까 broken down — consonant stem vs. vowel stem

먹eat-stem
으connector
니까because
가go-stem
니까because

먹으니까 ("since eating/eats") needs the connecting 으 because 먹 ends in a consonant (batchim ㄱ); 가니까 ("since going/goes") skips it because 가 already ends in a vowel.
