있다/없다 있다/없다

"There Is/Are", "To Have", and "To Be Located"

CategoryBasic Sentence Structure > 2. Basic Sentence Structure

1Concept Introduction — The Other "To Be" Verb

In GRAM-PRE-017 you learned 이다/아니다, the verb that links two nouns together — "A is B" or "A is not B." At the end of that chapter, you saw a quick preview: Korean actually has a second "to be"-type verb, and English speakers often reach for the wrong one. That second verb is 있다/없다, and this chapter is where you learn its rules in full.

Unlike 이다, which only ever connects two nouns, 있다 and its negative form 없다 describe something about a single noun: whether it exists, whether someone has it, or whether it is located somewhere. Because English uses "to be" for all of these ideas ("I am at school," "there is a book," "I have time"), English-speaking learners frequently try to force 이다 into sentences where Korean actually requires 있다. Getting this distinction right early will save you from one of the most common beginner mistakes in Korean.

Preview
사람이 있어요. (saram-i isseoyo, "There is a person.")
저는 시간이 있어요. (jeoneun sigan-i isseoyo, "I have time.")
저는 학교에 있어요. (jeoneun hakgyo-e isseoyo, "I am at school.")
One verb, three related meanings — existence, possession, and location. You will learn to tell them apart by context in this chapter.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 있다 and 없다 — meaning and polite casual form

있다 (itda) is the dictionary (base) form of a verb/adjective meaning "to exist," "to have," or "to be [located somewhere]." Its negation, 없다 (eopda), means "to not exist," "to not have," or "to not be [located somewhere]." Just like 이다/아니다, these base forms are conjugated before use in a sentence — for this beginner stage, you only need the polite casual form (해요체), which you will use in almost every everyday conversation.

있다/없다 in the polite casual form
Base FormMeaningPolite Casual FormRomanization
있다to exist / to have / to be located있어요isseoyo
없다to not exist / to not have / to not be located없어요eopseoyo
Study Tip Unlike 이다 (which attaches directly onto the noun before it, e.g. 학생이에요), 있다/없다 stand as their own separate word in the sentence, always coming after a noun marked with a particle such as 이/가 or 에. You will meet these particles in more depth soon, but for now just notice that 있다/없다 never attach directly onto the noun the way 이다 does.
