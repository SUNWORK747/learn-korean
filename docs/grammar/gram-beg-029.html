-(으)려고 Intention ("Intend To / In Order To")

Saying What You're Doing Something FOR — Intention Behind an Action

GRAM-BEG-029 Elementary (TOPIK I) 20 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급)
CategoryConnective Endings > 3. Connective Endings
Learning Goals ① Attach -(으)려고 to a verb stem to mean "intending to / in order to [verb]" — vowel-ending or ㄹ-batchim stems take -려고, consonant-batchim stems take -으려고
② Use -(으)려고 to connect two clauses that share the SAME subject: [intended action]-(으)려고 + [actual action taken], and recognize the second clause can contain any verb — unlike -(으)러, which requires a movement verb
③ Recognize that a -(으)려고 clause cannot be followed by an imperative (-(으)세요) or propositive (-(으)ㅂ시다) ending in the second clause

1Concept Introduction — Doing Something FOR a Reason

In GRAM-BEG-028 you learned -(으)러, which attaches to a verb stem to say you're moving somewhere in order to do something — but only with movement verbs like 가다 (to go) or 오다 (to come) in the second clause. -(으)려고 covers the same basic idea — intention, "in order to" — but it is far more flexible: the second clause can contain any verb, not just a movement verb. That makes -(으)려고 the more general, more frequently used tool of the two.

Structurally, -(으)려고 attaches to a verb stem using the same batchim rule you've seen before with endings like -(으)세요 and -(으)면: a consonant-ending stem takes -으려고, while a vowel-ending stem (or an ㄹ-batchim stem) takes -려고. The whole sentence pattern is [intended action]-(으)려고 + [actual action performed for that purpose], and both clauses must share the same subject.

Preview
저는 살을 빼려고 운동해요. ("I exercise in order to lose weight.")
친구를 만나려고 일찍 나갔어요. ("I left early in order to meet a friend.")
Notice the second clause verbs — 운동하다 (to exercise) and 나가다 (to leave/go out) — are not movement-to-a-destination verbs like 가다. That's exactly the flexibility -(으)려고 gives you.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Attaching -(으)려고 — the batchim rule

Just like -(으)세요 (GRAM-BEG-018) and -(으)러 (GRAM-BEG-028), whether you add 으 depends on whether the verb stem ends in a consonant (batchim) or not. A stem ending in ㄹ is treated like a vowel-ending stem — no 으 is added.

Verb stem + -(으)려고
Verb (dictionary form)Stem type+ -(으)려고Meaning
가다 (to go)vowel-ending가려고"intending to go"
먹다 (to eat)consonant batchim먹으려고"intending to eat"
빼다 (to lose/remove)vowel-ending빼려고"intending to lose (weight)"
만들다 (to make)ㄹ batchim만들려고"intending to make"
