-는 한 The Boundary of a Condition

"As Long As / Provided That" — a Limit That Must Keep Holding

GRAM-ADV-003 Advanced (TOPIK II) 35 min

1Concept Introduction — A Limit That Must Keep Holding

You already know -(으)면 ("if") from Elementary level (GRAM-BEG-027) as the all-purpose way to say "if X, then Y." -는 한 expresses something more specific and more formal: not just "if," but "as long as" — it marks a boundary condition that must keep being true for the result to keep holding. The moment that condition stops being true, the result is no longer guaranteed either. This is the language of essays, news articles, contracts, and academic writing: you'll see it constantly in written Korean, but rarely in casual speech.

The form is stricter than most connectives you've learned so far: -는 한 attaches only to the present-tense stem of verbs (and 있다/없다) — there is no past-tense version. Its negative counterpart, -지 않는 한, is one of the most common ways to say "unless" in formal Korean. This chapter covers the form rule, the core meaning of a persisting boundary, and how -는 한 differs from the simpler -(으)면 you already know.

Preview
최선을 다하는 한 후회는 없을 것이다. ("As long as one does one's best, there will be no regret.")
근본적인 대책이 마련되지 않는 한 문제는 해결되지 않을 것이다. ("Unless a fundamental measure is put in place, the problem will not be solved.")
다하는 한 sets doing-one's-best as the ongoing boundary condition for "no regret" — the moment that effort stops, the guarantee stops too. 마련되지 않는 한 is the negative form: "unless" a measure is prepared, the problem stays unsolved. Both sentences read like something from an essay or news report, not a casual chat.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: present-tense stem only — there is no past-tense version

-는 한 attaches directly to the present-tense stem of a verb, exactly the way -는 attaches in -는 것 (GRAM-BEG-038) or -는데 (GRAM-INT-001). 있다/없다 take the same -는 한 form. Unlike -는데, however, -는 한 has no past-tense form — you cannot say ☓했는 한 or ☓갔는 한. Descriptive adjectives generally don't combine with -는 한 either, with one important fixed exception: 가능하다 ("to be possible"), which appears as the frozen expression 가능한 한 ("as far as possible," using the adjective's own -(으)ㄴ ending, not -는).

-는 한 by word type
Word typeFormExampleNote
Verb (동사)-는 한지키다 → 지키는 한Present-tense stem only
있다/없다-는 한있다 → 있는 한 / 없다 → 없는 한Behaves like a verb
Negative-지 않는 한지키다 → 지키지 않는 한"unless"
가능하다 (fixed exception)-(으)ㄴ 한가능하다 → 가능한 한Frozen idiom, adjective form
Study Tip There is no -았/었는 한. If you feel tempted to mark the condition as "already finished," that's a signal -는 한 is the wrong tool — the whole point of -는 한 is an ongoing, not-yet-closed condition. Also remember that 가능한 한 ("as far as possible," e.g. 가능한 한 빨리 — "as quickly as possible") is a fixed phrase to memorize as-is, not a model for attaching -는 한 to other adjectives.
