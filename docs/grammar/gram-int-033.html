-(으)ㄴ 지 Elapsed Time Since an Action

How Long It's Been Since Something Happened

GRAM-INT-033 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 4th item of the "기타 중급 표현" (Other Intermediate Expressions) section, right after GRAM-INT-032 -는 바람에
CategoryOther Intermediate Expressions > 6. 기타 중급 표현
Learning Goals ① Understand that -(으)ㄴ 지 attaches to an action verb stem in its past-adnominal form and is always followed by a duration expression + 되다/지나다/넘다, to express "it has been [duration] since ~"
② Correctly form -(으)ㄴ 지 (batchim → -은 지, no batchim/ㄹ-irregular → -ㄴ 지, 하다 → 한 지), and recognize that 되다/지나다/넘다 can close the sentence in either past or present tense with the same meaning
③ Distinguish this bound-noun -지 (elapsed time, always written with a space) from the completely unrelated ending -지 used in indirect questions like -는지 (e.g. 뭐 하는지 몰라요), which is written with no space

1Concept Introduction — Saying How Long It's Been Since Something Happened

In English, you might say "I've lived here for three years" or "It's been three years since I moved here" — either way, the person doing the action tends to stay front and center. Korean has one dedicated pattern for this idea: -(으)ㄴ 지, attached to an action verb, always followed by a length of time and a closing verb like 되다 ("to become"), 지나다 ("to pass"), or 넘다 ("to exceed"). It's one of the most common ways Korean speakers ask and answer "How long has it been since...?" (e.g. 얼마나 됐어요?), so this pattern pays off in everyday conversation almost immediately.

The meaning itself isn't the hard part — two other things are. First, the sentence structure flips what actually "becomes" or "passes": in Korean, it's literally the duration of time that "becomes" or "passes," not the person. Second, -(으)ㄴ 지 sounds and looks exactly like a completely different, unrelated -지 used in indirect questions (뭐 하는지 몰라요, "I don't know what he's doing"). The two are total homophones with nothing in common in meaning, and this chapter will show you the one reliable trick for telling them apart.

Preview
한국어를 배운 지 6개월 됐어요. ("It's been six months since I started learning Korean.")
이 회사에서 일한 지 3년이 지났어요. ("Three years have passed since I started working at this company.")
배운 지 (learn + -(으)ㄴ 지) and 일한 지 (work + -(으)ㄴ 지) both attach to the past-adnominal form of the verb, then hand off to a duration + 되다/지나다 to say how much time has gone by since then.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: attaching -(으)ㄴ 지 to the verb stem

-(으)ㄴ 지 always attaches to an action verb stem (동사) — never to a descriptive adjective (형용사) — because the pattern specifically measures time since something happened, not since a state began. The attachment rule is the exact same past-adnominal -(으)ㄴ you already use before a noun (먹은 음식 "food [I] ate," 만난 사람 "person [I] met"): no batchim → -ㄴ 지; batchim → -은 지; ㄹ-irregular verbs drop the ㄹ before adding -ㄴ 지; and 하다 verbs become 한 지.

-(으)ㄴ 지 by verb stem type
VerbStem type-(으)ㄴ 지 form
가다 (to go)No batchim간 지
오다 (to come)No batchim온 지
먹다 (to eat)Batchim먹은 지
살다 (to live)ㄹ-irregular산 지
결혼하다 (to marry)하다 verb결혼한 지
Study Tip This is exactly the same -(으)ㄴ you already use to modify a noun with a past action (지난주에 만난 사람, "the person [I] met last week"). -(으)ㄴ 지 simply reuses that familiar form and attaches it to the bound noun 지 ("since") — so if you can already build a past-tense noun-modifying clause, you already know the hardest part of this pattern.
