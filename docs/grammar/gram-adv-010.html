-(으)나마 Such as It Is — A Resigned, Wistful Concession

Though It's Not What I Hoped For, At Least…

GRAM-ADV-010 Advanced (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelAdvanced (고급, TOPIK II 5–6급) — 4th item of the "양보·대조 고급 표현" (Advanced Concessive/Contrast Expressions) section, right after GRAM-ADV-009 -(으)ㄴ들
Category양보·대조 고급 표현 (Advanced Concessive/Contrast Expressions) > 2. 양보·대조 고급 표현
Learning Goals ① Understand -(으)나마 as a resigned, wistful concessive meaning "though it's not what one hoped for / such as it is, at least…" — used with both the very common noun + 이나마/나마 pattern (e.g. 잠깐이나마) and the verb/adjective stem + -(으)나마 pattern (e.g. 부족하나마)
② Correctly attach 이나마/나마 to a noun and -으나마/나마 to a verb or adjective stem based on whether the final syllable has 받침, and recognize common set expressions like 늦으나마, 부족하나마, 잠시나마
③ Distinguish -(으)나마 from GRAM-INT-027 -(이)라도 — both mark "settling for less than ideal," but -(으)나마 is a formal, written-register ending carrying explicit regret/wistfulness, while -(이)라도 is a noun particle used more broadly and neutrally across registers

1Concept Introduction — When "Not Enough" Is Still Enough

The concessives you've met so far in this section — -(으)ㄹ지라도, -더라도, -(으)ㄴ들 — all share the same logical shape: "no matter what happens in the first clause, the second clause holds anyway." -(으)나마 works differently. Instead of arguing against an obstacle, it concedes that something is brief, small, lacking, or imperfect, and then says the speaker accepts it, offers it, or is grateful for it anyway — with a distinct undertone of regret or wistfulness. The closest English equivalents are "though it's only…," "such as it is," "for what little it's worth," or "belated as it is."

You'll meet -(으)나마 in two shapes. By far the most common is the noun + 이나마/나마 pattern — 잠깐이나마 ("if only for a moment") and 잠시나마 ("if only for a while") are so frequent they function almost as fixed phrases. The second shape attaches directly to a verb or adjective stem — 부족하나마 ("lacking as it is"), 늦으나마 ("belated as it is") — and shows up constantly in formal speeches, thank-you notes, toasts, and reflective writing, wherever a speaker wants to humbly acknowledge that something falls short while still standing behind it.

Preview
잠깐이나마 만나서 반가웠어요. ("Though it was only a moment, I was glad to meet you.")
부족하나마 최선을 다했습니다. ("Though it was lacking, I did my best.")
Neither sentence argues that an outcome "holds no matter what." Instead, each one admits the thing in question wasn't quite enough — a brief meeting, an imperfect effort — and offers it anyway, gently and a little wistfully.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: Noun + 이나마/나마 — the pattern you'll see constantly

Just like 이에요/예요 and -(이)라도, the noun-attaching form of -(으)나마 splits according to whether the noun ends in a consonant (받침 있음) or a vowel (받침 없음). This is the shape you'll encounter most often in real Korean, usually with time-related nouns.

Noun + 이나마/나마
Noun endingFormExample
Consonant-final (받침 있음)이나마잠깐이나마, 몸이나마, 마음이나마
Vowel-final (받침 없음)나마잠시나마, 형식적으로나마
Study Tip 잠깐이나마 and 잠시나마 (both roughly "if only for a moment/a while") are so common in spoken and written Korean that it's worth memorizing them as ready-made chunks rather than building them from scratch each time.
