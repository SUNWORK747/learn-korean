-냐고 하다 Reported Speech — Questions

Asking What Someone Asked

1Concept Introduction — Reporting a Question Someone Asked

In GRAM-INT-020 you learned -다고 하다, the pattern for reporting what someone said as a statement. -냐고 하다 is its next-door neighbor: it reports what someone asked. Both belong to the same family of four quotation patterns — statement (-다고), question (-냐고), suggestion (-자고), and command (-(으)라고) — and they all share the exact same mechanics you already learned. You still need a reporting verb (most often 하다, but also 묻다 or 물어보다 when the sentence is specifically about asking), and you still drop the polite -아요/-어요 or formal -습니까 ending from the original sentence and replace it with the quotation ending — here, -냐고.

The good news is that -냐고 hardly changes shape at all: unlike -다고 (which splits into -는다고 for verbs and just -다고 for adjectives), -냐고 attaches directly to almost any stem — verb, adjective, or 있다/없다 — the same way. The only word type that needs a small adjustment is 이다, which becomes -(이)냐고. This chapter covers the full form chart, the difference between 하다/묻다/물어보다 as reporting verbs, and the everyday contracted form -냬요 that you'll hear constantly in spoken Korean.

Preview
친구가 저한테 "요즘 바빠요?" 라고 물어봤어요. (direct quote: "My friend asked me, 'Are you busy these days?'")
친구가 저한테 요즘 바쁘냐고 물어봤어요. ("My friend asked me if I was busy these days.")
The direct question 바빠요? loses its polite -아요 ending, and the plain adjective stem 바쁘- takes -냐고 instead: 바쁘냐고. The reporting verb 물어봤어요 ("asked") tells us this was a question, not a statement.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: attach -냐고 (하다) directly to the stem

For verbs, adjectives, and 있다/없다, -냐고 attaches straight to the plain stem — present tense needs no extra vowel insertion at all, past tense uses the -았/었- you already know, and future/guess uses -(으)ㄹ 거냐고. 이다 is the one exception: it becomes 이냐고 after a batchim, and can contract to just 냐고 after a vowel.

Key Rule Formal/written Korean still distinguishes -느냐고 (for verbs) from -(으)냐고 (for adjectives) — e.g. 가느냐고, 좋으냐고. But in everyday spoken Korean these have merged into one simple -냐고 that attaches directly to any stem, verb or adjective alike, with no extra vowel needed. This chapter teaches that modern, colloquial -냐고, since it's what you'll actually hear and use in conversation.
