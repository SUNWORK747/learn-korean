못/-지 못하다 Inability

"Cannot / Unable To" — Short Form vs. Long Form Inability

GRAM-BEG-031 Elementary (TOPIK I) 20 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — second item of this stage's Negation & Ability section
CategoryNegation & Ability > 4. Negation & Ability
Learning Goals ① Form short-form inability by placing 못 immediately before a verb (못 + verb), meaning "cannot / unable to" due to circumstance or lack of skill
② Form long-form inability by attaching -지 못하다 to a verb stem (stem + -지 못해요), structurally parallel to -지 않다 from GRAM-BEG-030
③ Distinguish 못 (inability) from 안 (simple negation/choice), and apply the same 하다-compound insertion rule (못 goes before 하다, not the whole word)

1Concept Introduction — "Cannot," Not Just "Don't"

In GRAM-BEG-030 you learned 안 and -지 않다 for plain negation — simply saying you don't do something, often by choice. 못 and -지 못하다 express something different: inability. They mean "cannot" or "unable to," implying the action is blocked by circumstance, lack of skill, or some outside reason — not just a simple choice not to do it.

Structurally, 못/-지 못하다 mirrors 안/-지 않다 almost exactly: a short form where 못 stands alone before the verb, and a long form where -지 못하다 attaches after the verb stem. If you're comfortable with GRAM-BEG-030, this chapter should feel like reusing the same skeleton with a different meaning layered on top.

Preview
저는 수영을 못 해요. ("I can't swim.")
저는 수영을 안 해요. ("I don't/won't swim.")
Same verb, very different meaning: 못 says swimming is impossible for the speaker (can't swim); 안 says the speaker simply chooses not to swim.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Short-form inability: 못 + verb

Just like 안, 못 is a standalone word placed directly in front of the conjugated verb. The verb's own conjugation doesn't change — you simply insert 못 right before it.

못 + conjugated verb
VerbConjugated못 + conjugatedMeaning
가다 (to go)가요못 가요"can't go"
먹다 (to eat)먹어요못 먹어요"can't eat"
보다 (to see)봐요못 봐요"can't see/watch"
자다 (to sleep)자요못 자요"can't sleep"
