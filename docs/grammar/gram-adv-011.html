1Concept Introduction — Despite the Fact That

This chapter closes out the five-part cluster on 양보·대조 고급 표현 (Advanced Concessive/Contrast Expressions) that began with GRAM-ADV-007. -(으)ㅁ에도 불구하고 is the strongest, most formal way Korean expresses "despite / in spite of the fact that" — and it stands apart from the other four patterns you've learned in this cluster in one important way. -(으)ㄹ지라도, -더라도, -(으)ㄴ들, and -(으)나마 are all concessions: they grant a hypothetical or lesser condition ("even if X were true…"). -(으)ㅁ에도 불구하고 is a contrast: it states that something is already, factually true — and then reports a surprising or opposite outcome anyway. That's why the item title calls it "강한 대조" (strong contrast), not "양보" (concession).

You've technically already met both halves of this pattern. -(으)ㅁ is the exact same nominalizer from GRAM-ADV-001 — the one that also stands alone at the end of formal sentences (이상 없음). Here, it attaches to a verb or adjective stem to turn a whole situation into a noun-like clause, which then takes the particle-like expression 에도 불구하고 ("in spite of," literally "even not being bound/held back by"). The result — stem + -(으)ㅁ + 에도 불구하고 — is everywhere in news reports, official statements, essays, and business writing: "despite having tried hard," "despite the government's efforts," "despite repeated warnings." Once you finish this chapter, the next category, 인과·조건 고급 표현 (Advanced Cause/Condition Expressions), begins with GRAM-ADV-012 -는 이상.

Preview
열심히 노력했음에도 불구하고 시험에 떨어졌다. ("Despite having studied hard, I failed the exam.")
정부의 지속적인 노력에도 불구하고 물가는 계속 상승하고 있다. ("Despite the government's continued efforts, prices continue to rise.")
Both sentences report a real, completed situation (studying hard; the government's actual efforts) and then state that the opposite of what you'd expect happened anyway. Compare that to 아무리 노력할지라도… ("no matter how hard I try…"), which concedes a hypothetical, not a fact.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: stem + -(으)ㅁ (GRAM-ADV-001) + 에도 불구하고

Because -(으)ㅁ here is the exact same nominalizer taught in GRAM-ADV-001, the shape-selection rule doesn't change at all: vowel-final and ㄹ-final stems take -ㅁ (the ㄹ drops and ㅁ becomes the new batchim), and consonant-final stems take -음. 에도 불구하고 then attaches after that nominalized clause, unchanged, no matter the stem type. To describe a completed or already-realized situation — by far the most common use in real writing — insert -았/었- before -음, giving -았/었음에도 불구하고.

Consonant-finalstem + -음 + 에도 불구하고있다 ("to exist/have")있음에도 불구하고

Past (any type)stem + -았/었 + -음에도 불구하고노력하다 ("to make an effort")노력했음에도 불구하고
Connecting Back to GRAM-ADV-001 There's nothing new to memorize about -(으)ㅁ itself here — it's the identical rule from GRAM-ADV-001: -ㅁ after vowels/ㄹ, -음 after consonants, -ㅁ after 이/아니. What's new is just what comes after it. Where GRAM-ADV-001 used -(으)ㅁ to end a sentence outright (이상 없음), this chapter uses it mid-sentence, as the noun-like clause that 에도 불구하고 attaches to.
