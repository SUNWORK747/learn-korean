② Correctly attach -(으)ㄹ지언정 to verb and adjective stems (vowel-final, consonant-final, ㄹ-final, and ㅂ/ㄷ irregulars) and to 이다/아니다, and recognize its typical two-part structure: (차라리) Clause A (an extreme or humbling concession) + -(으)ㄹ지언정, Clause B (a forceful, usually negative resolve, e.g. -지 않다/못하다)
③ Distinguish -(으)ㄹ지언정 from its near-synonym -(으)ㄹ망정 (GRAM-ADV-013): recognize -(으)ㄹ지언정 as the stronger, more formal, dramatic, and literary version — typical of speeches, literature, and rhetorical writing — while -(으)ㄹ망정 is comparatively more common in everyday advanced writing

1Concept Introduction — Even Though It May Be... (I Will Still...)

-(으)ㄹ지언정 closes out "담화·논증 표현" (Discourse/Argumentation Expressions), the final cluster of the advanced tier, right after GRAM-ADV-022 -기는커녕. It is the strongest, most dramatic member of the family of "defiant concession" patterns you have now met across the advanced level: neutral concessives like -더라도 and -(으)ㄹ지라도 simply say "even if A, B still happens," while -(으)ㄹ지언정 says something far more forceful — "granting that A may be true, even in its worst or most extreme form, I will absolutely still hold to B, and nothing will move me." Picture the classic declaration 굶을지언정 자존심을 팔지는 않겠다 ("Even though I may starve, I will never sell my pride") — Clause A concedes the harshest possible fate, and Clause B answers it with an unbending refusal. This is the register of speeches, literature, and rhetorical writing: patriotic vows, idealistic declarations, and moral stances that a speaker delivers almost as if standing at a podium.

Structurally and semantically, -(으)ㄹ지언정 is closest of all to -(으)ㄹ망정 (GRAM-ADV-013), which was flagged as "차라리 그럴지언정" back when that chapter was written. Both pair an unfavorable concession with a proud refusal and both are often glossed in English as "I would rather X than Y" — but -(으)ㄹ지언정 turns the intensity up further still, almost always opening with 차라리 ("rather") and closing Clause B with an explicit, forceful negative. Section 2.3 below lays out that comparison directly. And since this is the 118th and final item of the entire grammar curriculum — the last step after 22 beginner, 39 basic, 34 intermediate, and 23 advanced items — it feels fitting that the journey ends on Korean's most defiant, most resolute grammar pattern: a fitting note to close on, having built all the way up from 한글 itself to this.

Preview
차라리 굶을지언정 자존심을 팔지는 않겠다. ("Even though I may starve, I will never sell my pride.")
목숨을 잃을지언정 조국을 배신하지 않겠다. ("Even though I may lose my life, I will never betray my homeland.")
굶을지언정 (굶다, a consonant-final verb, + -(으)ㄹ지언정) and 잃을지언정 (잃다, a consonant-final verb, + -(으)ㄹ지언정) both concede the most extreme possible loss — then the second clause draws an unbending line the speaker will never cross.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: stem + -(으)ㄹ지언정

-(으)ㄹ지언정 attaches directly to verb and adjective stems, and to 이다/아니다, following the same shape rule as other -(으)ㄹ-family endings (like -(으)ㄹ지라도 and -(으)ㄹ망정): the 으 appears only when the stem ends in a consonant, a vowel-final stem takes -ㄹ지언정 directly, and a stem that already ends in ㄹ simply adds -지언정 (the extra ㄹ is never doubled). To concede an already-completed or decided situation, -았/었- can be inserted before it, giving -았/었을지언정 (e.g. 실패했을지언정, "even granting that [I] have already failed") — but as with -(으)ㄹ망정, this is far less common than the plain form, since the pattern most naturally frames an ongoing state or hypothetical extreme rather than a single finished event.

Study Tip 이다 becomes 일지언정 for every noun, whether or not it ends in a batchim — 거지일지언정 (거지 has no batchim) and 학생일지언정 (학생 has a batchim) follow the exact same pattern, exactly as with -(으)ㄹ망정. A ㄹ-final stem never doubles its ㄹ: 살다 becomes 살지언정, never 살ㄹ지언정. And like -(으)ㄹ망정, -(으)ㄹ지언정 leans heavily toward adjectives, 이다, and stative predicates, but it also comfortably takes strong action verbs describing an extreme, even fatal, outcome (죽다, 잃다, 굶다) — the more extreme the concession in Clause A, the more natural -(으)ㄹ지언정 sounds compared to its gentler cousin.
