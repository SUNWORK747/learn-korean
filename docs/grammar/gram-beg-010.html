(으)로 Means & Direction

By [Means Of], or Toward [Direction]

GRAM-BEG-010 Elementary (TOPIK I) 20 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급)
CategoryParticles > 1. Particles
Learning Goals ① Attach (으)로 correctly by batchim — 로 after a vowel-ending noun OR a noun ending in the ㄹ batchim (a special exception), and 으로 after any other consonant-ending noun
② Use (으)로 to mark the MEANS or INSTRUMENT by which an action is done ("by/with [means]")
③ Use (으)로 to mark DIRECTION ("toward")

1Concept Introduction — One Particle, Two Jobs

You've already met several particles that attach to nouns to show their grammatical role — 은/는 (topic), 이/가 (subject), 을/를 (object), and 에/에서 (time, location, starting point). (으)로 adds two more everyday jobs to that list: it can say "by/with" something (the means or tool used to do an action), or it can say "toward" somewhere (the direction something is heading).

Both uses attach the same way to the noun in front of them, so once you learn the attachment rule in this chapter, you can immediately use (으)로 for either meaning — context tells you whether it means "by this method" or "in this direction."

Preview
버스로 가요. ("[I] go by bus.") — here (으)로 marks MEANS: the bus is how the going happens.
이쪽으로 오세요. ("Please come this way.") — here (으)로 marks DIRECTION: "this side" is where the coming is headed.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Choosing 로 vs. 으로 — the batchim rule (with an exception to memorize)

Like 은/는, the choice between 로 and 으로 depends on how the noun in front ends. But (으)로 has one extra twist that other particles you've learned so far don't have: nouns ending in the ㄹ batchim behave like vowel-ending nouns here, not like other consonant-ending nouns.

(으)로 — attachment rule
Noun endingParticleExampleMeaning
Vowel (받침 없음)로버스로"by bus"
ㄹ batchim (exception!)로연필로"with a pencil"
Any other consonant (받침 있음)으로숟가락으로"with a spoon"
Key Rule — Memorize This Exception A noun ending in ㄹ batchim (like 연필, "pencil") takes 로, NOT 으로 — even though it ends in a consonant. This breaks the "consonant ending → 으로" pattern you'd otherwise expect, so treat vowel-ending OR ㄹ-batchim → 로 as one single rule, and every other consonant → 으로 as the other. There is no third option to worry about.
