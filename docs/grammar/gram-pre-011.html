Basic Pronunciation Rules — When a Plain Consonant Turns Tense

1Concept Introduction — The Last Piece of the Basic Pronunciation Set

Across GRAM-PRE-007 through GRAM-PRE-010 you learned that written Hangul and spoken Hangul are not always the same thing: batchim consonants link into the next syllable (linking), turn into nasal sounds before ㄴ/ㅁ (nasalization), fuse with ㅎ into an aspirated sound (aspiration), and even change place of articulation before 이 (palatalization). This chapter covers the last of the core batchim-driven rules: tensification (경음화) — the process that turns an ordinary, "plain" consonant into a tense one.

In GRAM-PRE-003 you already met the 5 tense consonants of Hangul: ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ. Tensification is the pronunciation rule that explains when a plain consonant written on the page — ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅅ, or ㅈ — actually gets pronounced out loud as one of those 5 tense sounds, even though the tense letter never appears in the spelling at all.

Preview
학교 (hakgyo, "school") is written with a perfectly plain ㄱ, but every Korean speaker pronounces it [학꾜] — the plain ㄱ after the batchim ㄱ automatically hardens into a tense ㄲ. Reading the spelling literally as "hak-gyo" would sound foreign; tensification is what makes it sound natively Korean.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Recap — The 5 tense consonants (GRAM-PRE-003)

Hangul has 5 tense consonants, each written by doubling a plain consonant letter: ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅃ, ㅆ, ㅉ. They are produced with tight vocal cords and no aspiration, giving a clipped, "hard" sound compared with their plain counterparts ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㅈ. Tensification does not add a new sound to the language — it simply explains one common situation in which a plain consonant is automatically pronounced as its tense partner.
