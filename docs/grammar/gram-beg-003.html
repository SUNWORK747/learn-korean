을/를 Object Particle

Marking the Direct Object of a Verb

GRAM-BEG-003 Elementary (TOPIK I) 15 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급) — third grammar item of this stage
CategoryParticles > 1. Particles
Learning Goals ① Attach 을/를 correctly based on batchim — 을 after a consonant-ending noun, 를 after a vowel-ending noun
② Use 을/를 to mark the direct object of a transitive action verb
③ Distinguish 을/를 (direct object of an action) from 에 (destination/location, taught in GRAM-BEG-005), avoiding the common learner error of using 을/를 with a movement verb's destination

1Concept Introduction — Naming What the Action Affects

You've now learned two particles that mark who or what a sentence is about: 은/는 (GRAM-BEG-001) marks the topic, and 이/가 (GRAM-BEG-002) marks the grammatical subject — the doer of the action. 을/를 completes this core trio by marking the direct object: the thing that receives or is affected by the action.

Just like every other particle you've met so far, 을/를 attaches directly to the end of a noun with no space, and which form you use is decided purely by batchim — the same consonant/vowel logic you already know from 은/는, 이/가, and 이에요/예요.

Preview
밥을 먹어요 (babeul meogeoyo, "eat rice") and 커피를 마셔요 (keopireul masyeoyo, "drink coffee") both mark the thing being eaten or drunk with 을/를 — but notice one attaches 을 and the other 를. The rule behind that difference is the first thing you'll learn in this chapter.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Choosing 을 vs. 를 — the batchim rule

을/를 attaches directly to the end of a noun. As with every particle pair you've studied so far, the choice depends only on whether the noun ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel.

을 vs. 를 — attachment rule
Noun endingParticleExampleMeaning
Consonant (받침 있음)을밥을"rice" (object)
Vowel (받침 없음)를커피를"coffee" (object)
Consonant (받침 있음)을책을"book" (object)
Vowel (받침 없음)를영화를"movie" (object)
Study Tip Same pattern, new particle: consonant-ending noun → the form starting with a consonant (을); vowel-ending noun → the form starting with a vowel (를). This is the fourth particle pair you've learned that works this exact way — by now the batchim rule should feel automatic.
