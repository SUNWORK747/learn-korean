도 Also/Too

A Particle That Replaces, Not Adds

GRAM-BEG-007 Elementary (TOPIK I) 15 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급)
CategoryParticles > 1. Particles
Learning Goals ① State that 도 means "also/too" and attaches directly to a noun
② Understand that 도 REPLACES 은/는, 이/가, 을/를 on that noun — it cannot co-occur with those particles on the same word
③ Correctly rewrite a sentence to add 도 in place of an existing topic/subject/object particle

1Concept Introduction — Saying "Also/Too"

You've now learned three of Korean's core particles: 은/는 (topic, GRAM-BEG-001), 이/가 (subject, GRAM-BEG-002), and 을/를 (object, GRAM-BEG-003). 도 (do) is another particle that attaches directly to a noun, and it means "also" or "too" — it says that this noun does the same thing, or has the same quality, as something already mentioned.

Here's the twist that makes 도 different from a simple "add-on" word like English "too": 도 doesn't sit alongside the particle already on the noun — it takes that particle's place. If a noun already has 은/는, 이/가, or 을/를 attached, and you want to say "also," those particles are removed and 도 goes in their spot. Nothing stacks.

Preview
저는 학생이에요. 친구도 학생이에요. ("I'm a student. My friend is ALSO a student.")
Notice 친구 does not carry 는 or 가 here — 도 has completely taken over that particle slot. This "replace, don't add" behavior is the single most important rule in this chapter.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 도 attaches directly — one form, no batchim rule

Unlike 은/는, which changes shape depending on whether the noun ends in a consonant or a vowel, 도 has only one form. It attaches directly to the end of any noun, with no space in writing, regardless of what sound the noun ends in.

도 attaching to different nouns
Noun+ 도RomanizationMeaning
저 (I)저도jeodo"me too / I also"
선생님 (teacher)선생님도seonsaengnimdo"the teacher too"
커피 (coffee)커피도keopido"coffee too"
학교 (school)학교도hakgyodo"school too"
Study Tip No batchim decision needed here — 도 is always just 도, whether the noun before it ends in a consonant (선생님도) or a vowel (저도). One less rule to memorize compared to 은/는.

3Examples

3.1 Replacing 은/는 and 이/가

In this pair of sentences, the second sentence adds a new person to the same statement — and 도 steps in exactly where a topic or subject particle would otherwise go.

Example
저는 학생이에요. 친구도 학생이에요. ("I'm a student. My friend is ALSO a student.")
친구 could have taken 가 (subject) or 는 (topic) in a plain sentence — but because we're saying "also," both are replaced by 도: 친구가 / 친구는 → 친구도.
