-지 마세요 Prohibition

"Please Don't...” — A Command, Not Just a Negation

GRAM-BEG-017 Elementary (TOPIK I) 15 min
LevelElementary (TOPIK I, 1–2급)
CategorySentence-Final Endings > 2. Sentence-Final Endings
Learning Goals ① Attach -지 마세요 to a verb stem to mean "please don't [verb]" — no batchim distinction needed, it always attaches the same way
② Use -지 마세요 to make a polite command/request that someone NOT do something
③ Distinguish -지 마세요 (a command not to do something) from 안/못 (GRAM-PRE-022, simple negation/inability) — a command vs. a statement

1Concept Introduction — Telling Someone Not To

In GRAM-PRE-022 you learned to say that something is not the case, or that you personally cannot do something, using 안 and 못. Those are both statements — they describe a fact or a limitation about the speaker. But what if you want to tell someone else not to do something? For that, Korean uses a completely different tool: -지 마세요.

-지 마세요 attaches to a verb stem to mean "please don't [verb]." It belongs to the imperative sentence type you previewed back in GRAM-PRE-015 (the four sentence-final types) — it is a polite command, not a description of fact. That single difference in function — command vs. statement — is the whole point of this chapter.

Preview
걱정하지 마세요. (geokjeonghaji maseyo, "Don't worry.") — a gentle, polite instruction telling the listener to stop or avoid an action, not a statement about the speaker at all.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Formation — verb stem + -지 마세요, no batchim rule

Unlike many endings you've met so far, -지 마세요 has no batchim distinction to worry about. You simply take the verb stem (the dictionary form minus 다) and attach -지 마세요 — every single time, regardless of what sound the stem ends in.

Verb stem + -지 마세요 (always the same attachment)
Dictionary formStem+ -지 마세요Meaning
걱정하다 (to worry)걱정하걱정하지 마세요"Don't worry."
피우다 (to smoke)피우피우지 마세요"Don't smoke."
늦다 (to be late)늦늦지 마세요"Don't be late."
가다 (to go)가가지 마세요"Don't go."
Study Tip Most endings you've learned so far change shape depending on batchim (은/는, 이에요/예요, etc.). -지 마세요 is refreshingly simple — it never changes. Stem + -지 마세요, always. One form, no exceptions.
