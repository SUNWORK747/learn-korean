-(이)나 Alternative Choice & Surprising Degree

"Or," and "As Many/Much As"

GRAM-INT-026 Intermediate (TOPIK II) 30 min
LevelIntermediate (TOPIK II, 3–4급) — 3rd item of the "조사·의존명사 확장" (Particles & Bound Nouns Expansion) section, right after GRAM-INT-025 -조차/마저
CategoryParticles & Bound Nouns Expansion > 5. 조사·의존명사 확장
Learning Goals ① Understand the two core uses of -(이)나: offering an alternative choice between nouns ("or"), and marking an unexpectedly large amount or degree ("as many/much as")
② Correctly attach 이나 after consonant-final nouns and 나 after vowel-final nouns, including the doubled -이나 -이나 pattern for open-ended, "whichever" lists
③ Distinguish this -(이)나 from question word + (이)나 free-choice forms (어디나, 누구나), which work like -든지 (GRAM-INT-003) rather than "or"/"as many as"

1Concept Introduction — Two Jobs, One Particle

-(이)나 is one of the most versatile particles in Korean — one small syllable that does two very different jobs depending on what it attaches to. Between two nouns, it works like "or," offering a casual, non-committal choice between options: "coffee or tea, whichever's fine." Attached to a number or quantity expression, though, it flips into something else entirely: a marker of surprise, showing that the speaker thinks the amount is bigger than expected — closer to English "as many as" or "a whole."

The form itself is simple — 이나 after a consonant-final noun, 나 after a vowel-final noun, the same sound-based pattern as 이/가 or 은/는. What takes more practice is reading context to tell which of the two meanings is in play, plus recognizing the doubled -이나 -이나 pattern Koreans use to list open-ended "whichever" options. This chapter also briefly compares -(이)나 to the free-choice forms built on question words (어디나, 누구나) that you already met with -든지 (GRAM-INT-003) — a related family, but a different job.

Preview
커피나 차 드릴까요? ("Would you like coffee or tea?")
사람들이 백 명이나 왔어요. ("As many as a hundred people came.")
커피나 (coffee + -나) offers an open, casual choice between two drinks. 백 명이나 (a hundred people + -이나) instead signals the speaker's surprise at how large the number is. Same particle, two very different effects.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: 이나 after consonants, 나 after vowels

Just like 이/가 or 을/를, the choice between 이나 and 나 depends only on sound — whether the noun ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel. There are no irregular exceptions to memorize here.

-(이)나 by noun ending
Noun endingParticleExample
Consonant-final (받침 O)이나빵이나 (bread, or…) / 밥이나 (rice, or…)
Vowel-final (받침 X)나커피나 (coffee, or…) / 학교나 (school, or…)
Study Tip This split is identical to the 이/가 pattern you already know: 받침 있으면 이나, 받침 없으면 나. No irregular verbs or sound changes to worry about — it's a pure, predictable sound-based rule.

3Examples

3.1 -(이)나 in real sentences: choice vs. surprising degree

KoreanMeaningFunction
