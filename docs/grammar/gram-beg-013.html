1Concept Introduction — A Rule You Already Know By Ear

Look back at literally any example sentence in this course so far — 저는 학생이에요 in GRAM-PRE-017, 저는 커피를 좋아해요 in GRAM-BEG-001, every single practice item and answer key. All of them end the same way: a polite, friendly, slightly informal tone that is neither stiff nor rude. That tone has a name — 해요체 (haeyoche, "polite casual style") — and a grammar rule behind it that you're about to learn for the very first time.

-아/어요 is the ending that creates 해요체. It attaches to the stem of a verb or adjective (the dictionary form minus 다) and turns it into a complete, polite sentence-ending you can use in everyday conversation — with friends, classmates, shopkeepers, coworkers, almost anyone who isn't a large formal audience. Which of the two forms, -아요 or -어요, you attach depends on one simple vowel rule.

Preview
가다 (gada, "to go") → 가요 (gayo, "[I] go"), but 먹다 (meokda, "to eat") → 먹어요 (meogeoyo, "[I] eat") — same ending family, two different vowels. The rule that decides between them is exactly what this chapter teaches.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 What 해요체 is, and why you've already been using it

Korean has several sentence-final "speech styles" (registers) that signal politeness and formality. 해요체 is the most common one in daily life: polite enough for strangers and elders, relaxed enough for friends. Because this course is written for real spoken use, every model sentence from GRAM-PRE-001 onward has been in 해요체 — you were absorbing its sound and rhythm long before learning its name. This chapter finally opens the hood and teaches the conjugation rule that produces it.

To build -아/어요, first remove 다 from the dictionary form to get the stem. Then look at the last vowel of that stem — that one vowel decides everything.
