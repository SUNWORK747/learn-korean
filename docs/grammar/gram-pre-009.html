Aspiration 격음화

Basic Pronunciation Rules — When ㅎ Merges With a Neighboring Consonant

GRAM-PRE-009 Beginner (Pre-TOPIK) 20 min
LevelBeginner (for students who already know the Hangul consonants and the batchim/nasalization rules)
CategoryHangul > 1. Hangul (Alphabet & Pronunciation)
Learning Goals ① State the aspiration rule: when ㅎ meets one of ㄱ/ㄷ/ㅂ/ㅈ — in either order — the two merge into a single aspirated sound [ㅋ]/[ㅌ]/[ㅍ]/[ㅊ]
② Apply the rule to read real words correctly in both directions (ㅎ-first and ㅎ-second)
③ Distinguish aspiration from nasalization (GRAM-PRE-008): aspiration always specifically involves ㅎ, while nasalization is triggered by a following ㄴ or ㅁ

1Concept Introduction — When ㅎ Meets a Plain Consonant

In GRAM-PRE-008 you learned that a batchim consonant can change its sound when the syllable after it starts with ㄴ or ㅁ (nasalization). There is a second, very common batchim change that has nothing to do with ㄴ or ㅁ: it happens whenever the consonant ㅎ sits right next to one of ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅈ — whether ㅎ comes first (as a batchim) or second (as the next syllable's initial consonant). This is called aspiration (격음화), because the two consonants do not just influence each other — they physically fuse into one single aspirated ("breathy") sound.

Aspiration is one of the most frequent sound changes in spoken Korean because ㅎ is common both as a batchim (especially in verb/adjective stems like 좋다 "to be good") and as the first sound of very common words and suffixes (like 하다-based words, or -하다/학 in nouns). Once you know this single rule, you can correctly pronounce dozens of everyday words that look tricky on the page but are actually simple once you hear the merge happen.

Preview
좋다 (jota, "to be good") is pronounced [조타] — the batchim ㅎ and the following ㄷ merge into a single ㅌ sound. And 축하 (chukha, "congratulations") is pronounced [추카] — here the batchim ㄱ and the following ㅎ merge into a single ㅋ sound. Same rule, opposite order.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 The core rule — ㅎ + plain consonant → aspirated consonant

Korean has three "versions" of several consonant sounds: plain (ㄱ/ㄷ/ㅂ/ㅈ), tense (ㄲ/ㄸ/ㅃ/ㅉ), and aspirated (ㅋ/ㅌ/ㅍ/ㅊ) — the aspirated versions are pronounced with an extra puff of breath. Aspiration (격음화) is the rule that says: whenever ㅎ is directly adjacent to one of the four plain consonants ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅈ, the two sounds merge into the matching aspirated consonant — regardless of which one comes first.

The four aspiration merge pairs
Plain consonantMerges with ㅎ (either order) to becomeMerged sound
ㄱㅎ+ㄱ or ㄱ+ㅎ[ㅋ]
ㄷㅎ+ㄷ or ㄷ+ㅎ[ㅌ]
ㅂㅎ+ㅂ or ㅂ+ㅎ[ㅍ]
ㅈㅎ+ㅈ or ㅈ+ㅎ[ㅊ]
Key Rule Aspiration only ever involves ㅎ. If you see ㅎ touching ㄱ/ㄷ/ㅂ/ㅈ across a syllable boundary — in either order — expect the two letters to collapse into one aspirated sound when spoken.
