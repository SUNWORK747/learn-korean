-다시피 하다 Nearly / Practically the Same as Doing

Exaggerating a Habit, and the Related "As You Know" Connective

1Concept Introduction — "Practically" Doing Something

-다시피 하다 is a compact way of exaggerating just how close an action came to being literally true. When a Korean speaker says 매일 야근하다시피 해요 ("These days I practically work overtime every day"), they don't necessarily mean every single day without exception — they mean it's close enough to that extreme that it's worth describing it that way. This "almost, but let's say it as if it were" nuance is what makes -다시피 하다 distinct from simply stating the action plainly. It shows up constantly in personal storytelling, casual advanced conversation, and in writing whenever a speaker wants to underline how intense, constant, or extreme a habit or situation was.

The same -다시피 building block also appears on its own, without 하다, in a very common formal expression: -다시피, most often attached to 알다, 보다, or 듣다, to mean "as you know," "as you can see," or "as you've heard." Both uses share the core idea of "as if X were the case" — one applies it to an exaggerated action, the other to a fact the listener already shares. This chapter covers both, since they're built from the same morpheme but do different jobs in a sentence. It's also the final item of the "문어체·격식 표현" (Formal/Written Expressions) section — the next chapter, GRAM-ADV-007, opens a new section on 양보·대조 고급 표현 (Concessive/Contrast Advanced Expressions).

Preview
저는 요즘 회사에서 살다시피 해요. ("These days I practically live at the office.")
아시다시피 이 프로젝트는 다음 주까지 끝내야 해요. ("As you know, this project has to be finished by next week.")
살다시피 해요 (live + -다시피 하다) exaggerates how much time the speaker spends at the office — not literally living there, but close enough. 아시다시피 (알다 + -시- + -다시피) instead references something the listener already knows, before making the main point. Same -다시피, two different jobs.

2Concept Explanation

2.1 Form: Verb stem + -다시피 하다

-다시피 하다 attaches directly to an action verb (동사) stem, in both present and past tense, with no variation based on the final consonant (batchim) of the stem — unlike many other endings you've studied, there's no -(으)- alternation to worry about here. It does not attach to adjectives (형용사), since the whole point is describing an exaggerated action, not a state or quality.

-다시피 하다: present and past tense
TensePatternExampleMeaning
PresentVerb stem + -다시피 하다매달리다 → 매달리다시피 하다"practically cling to / beg"
PastVerb stem + -다시피 했다매달리다 → 매달리다시피 했다"practically clung to / begged"
PresentVerb stem + -다시피 하다굶다 → 굶다시피 하다"practically starve"
PastVerb stem + -다시피 했다굶다 → 굶다시피 했다"practically starved"
Study Tip Because -다시피 하다 attaches straight to the stem with no batchim rule, it's one of the easier advanced forms to produce — the real challenge is knowing which verbs it sounds natural with. It shows up most often with verbs describing routines, physical states, or emotional extremes: 살다 (live), 굶다 (starve), 뛰다 (run), 기다 (crawl), 매달리다 (cling/beg), 새우다 (stay up all night), 외우다 (memorize).
